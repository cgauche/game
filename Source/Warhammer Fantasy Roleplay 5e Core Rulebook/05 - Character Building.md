# <span id="page-21-0"></span>• **CHARACTER BUILDING** •

You'll experience a life of adventure in the Empire through the eyes of your Character. Follow the steps here to create your Character using a combination of game information describing their abilities, and the personality and background story you invent for them.

A Character's game information comes from the decisions and dice rolls you make as you create them. This process also suggests features to weave into your Character's story, but the details of who they are and the experiences that shape them are yours to create. The chapter on the Reikland (see page 272) provides useful setting details to build into your Character's background. Including elements of lore, even simple things like choosing a town for them to have grown up in, will help make them feel like an inhabitant of the world.

As you make your Character, you will record everything about them on your Character Sheet. On the next page we explain all the parts of the Character Sheet, what goes where, what it means, and where that information comes from. There's a blank Character Sheet at the end of the book and on www.cubicle7games.com — there are also some ready-made Characters complete with pre-filled sheets online if you'd rather learn how the game works before making your own Character.

Your Character needs to work well with the other PCs and fit into the game the GM has planned, so talk as a group about your Character plans. For example, it would be hard to investigate a rogue wizard if none of the Characters can detect the use of magic!

# **CHARACTER SHEET EXPLAINED**

<span id="page-23-0"></span>This sheet is where you keep track of your Character's abilities, equipment and more. Characteristics and Skills are often used to find out how well you perform an action. You will roll the dice (the two ten-sided dice known as D100, see page 6) and if the result is equal to or lower than the relevant Skill, your action succeeds. **The higher the value of your Skill, the easier it is to succeed**.

Other factors such as the difficulty of the task impact this too (see page 130) but for now it's enough to know that your Skill number is the basic chance of succeeding at a Challenging task.

**Prioritise the Skills you want your Character to be best at by placing the highest values and most Advances in those Skills and their associated Characteristics.**

- **Personal Details:** Name, Species (page 23), Appearance
- **Experience:** Your Gamemaster will award Experience Points, also called XP, ) you use to improve your Character's abilities (see page 191. Keep a record of your unspent, spent, and total XP.
- **Movement:** how quickly you can move (see page 40).
- **Fate and Fortune:** Help you succeed and survive (see page 133).

**Class and Career:** Your occupation (see page 36). Status (Brass/Silver/Gold) and Standing (your rank within your Status, the higher the better) show your wealth and position (page 140).

The Career Advancement Tracker tracks your progress through your Career. Each time you Advance a Skill or Characteristic, purchase a Talent, or gain a Trapping from a higher Career Level, tick a box. Once all the boxes before a numbered skull are ticked, you can increase your Career Level (page 43).

**Characteristics:** Your innate abilities, and the starting score for your Skills. The first digit (the 'tens' value) is your **Characteristic Bonus**, used in many rules. For example, your **Strength Bonus (SB)** is added to the damage you deal with melee attacks. Strength **37** gives an **SB of 3**, while **42** gives an **SB of 4**.

- 0 **Weapon Skill:** aptitude for fighting handto-hand, with or without a weapon.
- 0 **Ballistic Skill:** effectiveness with ranged weapons like bows and throwing knives.
- 0 **Strength:** damage caused in fights, lifting weights, swimming and climbing.
- 0 **Toughness:** physical hardiness, resisting damage, surviving harsh conditions and poisons.
- 0 **Initiative:** thought/reaction speed, especially in battle or under pressure.
- 0 **Agility:** coordination and athleticism, used in running, riding, leaping, or dodging.
- 0 **Dexterity:** performing delicate manual tasks like playing a musical instrument or skilled manufacturing.
- 0 **Intelligence:** powers of thought, analysis, and understanding. Useful for healing, evaluating, and general knowledge.
- 0 **Willpower:** strength of mind, determination and focus.
- 0 **Fellowship:** making a good impression and getting on with people.

**Skills:** learned and developed abilities. A Skill starts at the value of its associated Characteristic (shown on the Character Sheet and/ or the entry for the relevant Skill in Chapter 4, page 110). **Each Advance increases it by +5**. Your starting Skills come from your Species and Career.

#### **There are three types of Skill:**

- 0 Basic Skills can be used by anyone, even without any Advances.
- 0 Advanced Skills can only be used if you have at least one Advance in the Skill.
- 0 Grouped Skills cover broad areas. When you take an Advance, choose a specialization such as Lore (Empire) or Lore (Tilea).

- **Armour:** record your Armour (page 307) here, and write the total Armour Points (APs) in the Armour diagram on the front of the sheet.
- **Corruption and Mutation:** exposure to Chaos leads to Corruption and Mutation (page 187), but your Character begins the game without such curses.
- **Trappings, Encumbrance, and Wealth:** the equipment, clothes, weapons, and other items you carry. You gain starting **Trappings** from your Class and Career, and can buy or find more during play (see page 297). Record each item's **Encumbrance** and make sure you can carry it all (see page 299). **Wealth** is measured in brass pennies, silver shillings, and gold crowns.
- **Spells and Prayers:** magical or religious Careers give access to Spells (see page 240) or Prayers (see page 220).
- **Talents:** special abilities, tricks and techniques with individual game impacts (see page 114).
- **Ambition:** your Character's goals and dreams (see page 41). Party Ambition is what keeps your party together — the goal or agenda you share in common.
- **Wounds:** how much damage you can take before suffering Critical Injuries. Your starting maximum Wounds are calculated from your **Strength Bonus**, **Toughness Bonus (doubled)**, **Willpower Bonus**, and any other modifiers, such as the *Hardy* Talent (see page 40).

Damage reduces your Wounds, while Healing restores them, up to your maximum (see page 170). Increasing your Characteristics or gaining certain Talents can increase your maximum Wounds. **If you take Damage when you have 0 Wounds, you suffer a Critical Injury** (see page 171). Record any Critical Injuries and their effects in the large box.

**Armour:** Write the total Armour Points (APs) in each location on the diagram. Armour Points reduce the damage you take from weapons.

# <span id="page-25-0"></span>**HUMANS (REIKLANDERS)**

Reiklanders come from the heart of the Empire and are its most numerous people. They tend to be friendly, open, and optimistic, with the confidence that comes from living in the province that gave birth to Sigmar, the Empire's patron god. To outsiders, however, that confidence often appears as arrogance, and Reiklanders have a reputation for meddling in other people's affairs, convinced they know best.

Like all humans, Reiklanders live shorter lives than the elder species, but make up for it with vigour, versatility, and ambition. Humanity's rise over the last few millennia has seen it become the dominant power in the Old World, yet it also has an unsettling affinity for the Ruinous Powers. More humans succumb to corruption than any other species, leaving many elves and dwarfs to wonder whether humanity's greatest strength may also prove its downfall.

Human civilisations have existed in distant lands for thousands of years, but the Empire's people trace their ancestry to tribes that migrated to the Old World after the disastrous war between the elves and dwarfs. The Unberogen tribe settled the Reikland, and their greatest son, Sigmar, united the tribes to found the Empire. His coronation marks year one of the Imperial Calendar, and he is said to have ascended to godhood 50 years later. Today, temples and shrines to Sigmar stand in every settlement, and most Reiklanders are devout believers in his message of unity.

# <span id="page-27-0"></span>**DWARFS**

Dwarfs are tough, resourceful, proud, and honourable. Given their long memories and proud natures, they bear grudges against those who have dishonoured them, even taking on the grievances of their ancestors. They believe their forebears watch over them, nursing bitterness long after death. Whilst it might be hard to win a dwarf 's friendship, once given, it is sincere and lasting.

They are squat and powerfully built, with thick hair and heavy features. The length of a dwarf 's beard is a mark of pride and status, and to shave it is a terrible shame. While not as ageless as the near-immortal elves, dwarfs can live for many centuries. Indeed, some say that as long as a dwarf has a purpose, they will not die unless struck down in battle.

Dwarfs are encountered throughout the Old World, although most reside in their holds, vast fortified mines dug deep beneath the mountains. For all their prowess in battle and expertise in craftsmanship, their civilisation is a shadow of its former glory and continues to decline. They often speak their own language, khazalid, but are reluctant to teach it to others. They use the name Karaz Ankor — meaning Everlasting Realm — to refer both to their ancient mountain empire and to the beleaguered holds that remain under dwarf control. The dwarf calendar begins in the year –4523 IC, when the Great Hall of Karaz-a-Karak was first established, and dwarfs still pay respect to the high king who sits upon its throne, though for those living in the Empire, he is a distant presence.

# <span id="page-29-0"></span>**HALFLINGS**

Halflings are cheerful, sociable, and possessed of enormous appetites in all things. They care more for good food and strong drink than matters of politics or war, and are notorious across the Empire as thieves, poachers, and scoundrels. Halflings regard unattended property as fair game and see little shame in taking advantage of the careless. Most are quick with a joke, quicker with an excuse, and quicker still with someone else's coin. Even so, they are fiercely loyal to their kin, and few are more generous when surrounded by family, friends, and a wellstocked table.

They are short, apple-cheeked and beardless, resembling bigeyed, round-faced (and round-bodied) human children. Their harmless appearance and easy smiles encourage others to underestimate them — a misconception halflings happily cultivate. Many a traveller has discovered, too late, that the friendly halfling offering directions, sharing a pipe, or buying the first round has picked their pockets while doing so.

The first records of halflings come from the fertile lands where the Blue Reach meets the Aver, around 200 years before the birth of Sigmar. Most still dwell there today, in an area named The Moot after their fondness for settling matters through garrulous mass assembly. The Elder of the Moot, currently Hisme Stoutheart, is custodian of the Haffenlyver, an ancient embroidered scroll recording the lineages of the great halfling clans. They can be found throughout the Empire, however, and Haffenstadt, a district of Altdorf, is packed with halfling families supporting restaurants, taverns, pipeweed stores, and an astonishing number of street-food hawkers. Halflings in more rural areas tend to make their living by running inns, poaching game, or smuggling illicit cargoes.

# <span id="page-31-0"></span>**HIGH ELVES**

High elves, who call themselves the Asur, are among the oldest civilisations in the world. Proud, cultured, and intensely disciplined, they use ritual and tradition to keep their soaring emotions in check and provide focus for their complicated intellects. They often seem aloof or arrogant to other species, and still harbour disdain for dwarfs after the terrible War of the Beard. Yet not all Asur are solemn nobles. Some revel in adventure, taking to the seas as mariners, traders, and diplomats. In particular, the adventurous 'sea elves' are often found trading with other species, and exploring the world's oceans.

Tall and slender with delicately pointed ears, elves have long, fine hair and melodious voices. Although they appear frail, they are surprisingly strong and extraordinarily agile and dextrous. Male and female elves look much alike, often causing confusion among humans.

Ulthuan, an island continent hundreds of miles west of the Old World, is the homeland of the elves. Its legends tell how the land was formed from the corpse of Indraugnir, father of dragons. Five thousand years before Sigmar, the hero Aenarion passed through the sacred fires of Asuryan, creator god of the elves, becoming the first phoenix king and establishing the twin thrones of Ulthuan with the Everqueen Astarielle. After the death of Caledor II in –1600 IC, the high elves withdrew from the Old World and spent centuries in relative isolation while their society was riven by civil war, the Kinstrife, a subject they seldom discuss with outsiders.

# <span id="page-33-0"></span>**WOOD ELVES**

Wood elf life is entwined with nature, and their society is intermixed with the spirits of the forest. They are separatists whose arboreal outposts are concealed by misleading illusions. On the rare occasions they venture abroad, it is usually for war, as often with their neighbours as with darker forces, leaving the peoples of the Old World unsure where the allegiance of these fae, capricious elves truly lies.

As the War of the Beard ended, most elves retreated from the Old World, but a few remained in the depths of the forests they called home. More than four thousand years of isolation, hardship, and warfare left them culturally distinct from their high elf cousins. Individuals from two kiths of wood elves are occasionally encountered in the Empire: the Asrai of Athel Loren, beyond the Grey Mountains, and the elves who dwell in the Laurelorn Forest in Nordland.

The Asrai are xenophobic, secretive, and seldom venture abroad. A decade ago, their venerated seer, Naith the Prophetess, received visions foretelling the destruction of Athel Loren. In response, the king and queen of the wood dispatched warrior kinbands to hunt their foes beyond the forest. These ferocious bands are sometimes led by a spellsinger who uses the magical paths of the Worldroot to travel between Athel Loren and distant forests. Occasionally, these hunters make common cause with other peoples of the Old World, and an individual may step from the shadows to join those confronting a shared enemy.

The elves of the Laurelorn are more consistent than the wild Asrai and even honour treaties with their human neighbours. Their queen, Marrisith, heard Naith's prophecy and sent a sizeable delegation to establish a camp in the deep forest of the Amber Hills, south of Altdorf. These elves observe human politics concerning the Laurelorn and beyond, intervening only in matters they deem important.

Marrisith's envoys reveal little of their purpose, and strike the Reikland's human inhabitants as uncanny, though they tend to be more approachable than the Asrai.

### **SYLVAN COUSINS**

The Wood Elves of Athel Loren and the Laurelorn may share a common history and origin in the Old World, but they are culturally different. Unlike the high elves, neither of the wood elven kiths care much for any form of conventional civilisation, seeing it as a threat to their beloved forests and way of life. They differ in how they choose to express this, however.

The Laurelorn wood elves proactively engage in politics to protect themselves, mixing with others, and are not above stoking the fires of hatred between other groups to shift focus to their preferred targets. Thus, in character, they are more like the high elves, whilst still having the Skills and Talents of the wood elves.

By comparison, the Athel Loren kinbands prefer to hide in the shadows, shunning contact with all others and instead striking deadly blows designed to cause fear and terror in the hearts of any who would look to the forest with greed.

# <span id="page-39-0"></span>**Wounds**

Wounds show how much Damage you can take before you suffer serious injury.

**Wounds = Strength Bonus (SB) + Willpower Bonus (WPB) + 2 × Toughness Bonus (TB)**

So, if you have **3**4 Strength, **2**7 Willpower and **3**8 Toughness, your Wounds would be 3+2+3+3=11. If you have the Hardy Talent, add your Toughness Bonus again.

Write your Wounds total on your Character Sheet.

# **Movement (M)**

Movement is used to determine your Walk and Run speed. If using a grid for movement, this indicates how many squares you can move in a Round. If not using a grid, it indicates how many inches across the tabletop. When you Run, you double these distances.

**Example:** *A human with a Movement of 4 can walk 4 squares or run 8 squares per Round on a grid. If not using a grid, they can walk 4 inches or run 8 inches per Round.*

Over the longer term it indicates how many miles per hour you can comfortably travel on foot. For more on Movement, see page 156, and 162 for moving in comabt.

This measures how much you can comfortably carry without being slowed down and tired out.

If you are creating a Character who is a spellcaster or miracle worker of some sort, bear in mind that you will need to meet a short list of minimum requirements in order to work your magic from the start of your adventuring career.

A spellcaster has various options in order to cast a Spell, such as reading from grimoires, but a sensible starting suite of abilities would include:

- 0 The *Second Sight* Talent, without which you may not perceive the Winds of Magic.
- 0 At least one advance in the Language (Magick) Skill.
- 0 At least one advance in the Channelling Skill, with a specialisation appropriate to the type of magic you wish to work with. For example, a Wizard using the Lore of Shadows would need Channelling (*Ulgu*).
- 0 The *Petty Magic* Talent, in order to begin learning spells to cast.

This may be quite a lot to acquire as a starting character, but once you have the necessary Skills and Talents you will be able to increase your repertoire of spells when you gain experience. See **Chapter 8: Magic** for details of the effects of Spells and how to cast them.

Priests have a slightly easier time, reflecting the fact that their Blessings and Miracles are not as varied and powerful as Spells. In order to begin play as a Priest capable of working Blessings a Character will need:

- 0 The *Bless (Deity)* Talent, appropriate to the god you have chosen to worship.
- 0 At least one advance in the Pray Skill.

See **Chapter 7: Religion and Belief** for details of Blessings, and how to work Miracles.

# **AND BACKGROUND**

## **Who are You?**

Fleshing out your Character's personality and history will really help you bring them to life. Some players write detailed backstories for their Character, others prefer to just decide the basics at the start and improvise during the game (discuss this with the GM beforehand so you don't derail any storylines).

## **Choose an Ambition**

An Ambition is your Character's goal in life — what they want to achieve. It helps define what is important to your Character, and making progress towards or achieving your Ambition earns additional XP.

Choose a long-term goal your Character may work towards for months or even years, and may even never achieve. Discuss your Ambition with your GM to make sure it is significant enough and fits your game. If inspiration strikes, you can change your Ambition between sessions.

#### Examples include:

- 0 Owning your own coaching inn.
- 0 Building your village into a thriving town.
- 0 Ridding the Colleges of Magic of Elven influence.

## **Achieving Your Ambitions**

**6. PERSONALITY** II At the end of any session in which you make genuine progress towards your ambition, the GM should reward you with an additional 50 XP. This should be real, measurable, and substantial progress. Killing one of the five mercenaries who slew your parents is real progress towards a vengeful Ambition. Buying a sword with the intent of going after them is not.

> Achieving your Ambition rewards you with an additional 250 XP. This is a moment to decide if your Character's story has reached its end, and if you'd prefer to retire them and continue your campaign with a new Character. If not, choose a new Ambition that gets your Character back out into the world and facing down its dangers.

## **What Brings You Together?**

At the start of Character creation you discussed the kind of group you wanted to create (see **Fitting In**, page 22). Now you know who your Characters are, you can add some more details of how and why they associate with each other. Some ways to do this are:

- 0 Weaving your Characters into each others' backstories, building their shared history and reasons for sticking together.
- 0 Describe how your Character knows the Characters of the players sitting to your left and right.

## **QUESTIONS**

One useful technique for creating an interesting background for your Character is to answer a series of questions about them.

#### **Where are you from?**

Do you come from one of Reikland's bustling towns, or a sleepy village? Perhaps your childhood was spent on a remote farmstead, or maybe you called the winding back alleys of Altdorf home? See **Chapter 10: Glorious Reikland** for details on the geography and settlements of this corner of the Empire.

#### **What is your family like?**

What were your parents' occupations? Are they still alive? Do you have any siblings? Are they proud of you, or are you a stain on your family's honour? Any dark family secrets the witch hunters might be interested in? Do you still live where you grew up, or did you leave, and why?

#### **Who are your best friends?**

Do you have any friends from childhood? What do they do now, and where do they live? Did you make any friends pursuing your Career? Or is your small but vicious dog the only true friend Sigmar has blessed you with?

#### **What are your best and worst memories?**

What are your most treasured memories? That afternoon a barrel of Bugmans fell unnoticed from the merchant's wagon, or a victory scored in a battle of wits or brawn? And what of your less fond recollections?

#### **What are your religious beliefs?**

Does your character have a fondness or affinity for one god in particular? Do you cross your fingers for luck, appealing to Ranald, or make the sign of the Twin Tailed Comet at your enemies? See **Chapter 7: Religion and Belief** for details of the gods and their ways.

#### **To whom, or what, are you loyal?**

Will you protect your friends above all, or are the ties that bind you to your clan or kinfolk stronger than iron? Or are you a pious soul, dedicated above all to your god? Are you loyal to your community, to Reikland, the Empire… or just to yourself?

#### **What have you been doing lately?**

Have you been conscientiously applying yourself to your Career? Do you have any enemies or grudges to settle? Is the acquisition of money and possessions important to you, or do you pursue happiness or spiritual contentment?

## **Party Ambition**

A Party Ambition is a long-term goal shared by the whole group, which binds you together. It might be based on shared beliefs or a common enemy, or be as practical as earning a living.

Discuss your Party Ambition with the GM as a group, to make sure it fits the campaign. Unlike a personal Ambition, making progress towards a Party Ambition grants no XP. Completing it, however, gives the incredibly rare reward of an additional Fate Point for each Character.
