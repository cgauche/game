To his Imperial Majesty, Emperor Karl Franz the First, by the Grace of the Gods, Elector Count and Grand Prince of Reikland, Prince of Altdorf, Count of the West March, Defender of Sigmar's Faith, do I commend this text, an examination of his most illustrious realm, the Grand Princedom of Reikland, heartland of Our Holy Empire. Long may he rule!

Herein learn of this most mighty province, and of its hard-working people, united by the worship of the God-King Sigmar. Admire the grand princes of the Reikland who have led our nation for nigh on a century, and their generous patronage that enriches our homeland beyond measure. Wonder at the Reikland's impressive canals, extensive roadworks, bounteous farms, and thriving commerce, all of which confirm the Reikland as a true jewel of the Empire.

Marvel at this richest, most cosmopolitan of all Imperial realms, and see that it is a cultural, magical, and academic powerhouse without par, attracting the most accomplished artisans, wizards, and scholars to its many learned institutions, assuring its reputation as the greatest domain of the Old World.

Truly, to be born a Reiklander is to be born blessed by the Gods Themselves. Give thanks to Sigmar and be praised.

Standing proud in the heart of the Old World, Reikland is the richest and most powerful of the Empire's grand provinces. Known for its engineers, wizards, and merchants, and for being the birthplace of the Cult of Sigmar, it is a realm of soaring mountains, snaking rivers, dark forests, and powerful trading centres. From his throne in the city of high-spired Altdorf, Emperor Karl Franz rules not only the Reikland, but all the Empire that lies beyond.

# **THE LIE OF THE LAND**

The Reikland is named for the largest river in the Old World, the majestic Reik, which winds through the western Empire and defines the province's eastern and northern borders. The land is blanketed by dense forests, which overshadow the countless settlements, rivers, and roads that cut through the trees.

Much of the land near the Reik is waterlogged and marshy, with long stretches of bogs, swamps, and treacherous mud flats. Away from the wetlands, a multitude of prosperous towns, villages, and cities cling to the riverbanks.

Farther inland, the Reikland climbs upwards to the craggy Skaag Hills and the haunted Hägercrybs, forested uplands hiding isolated regions where one can travel for days without seeing another person. To the west, the forbidding Grey Mountains cast their shadow over the forest-covered lowlands. On the lower slopes, the trees give way to a wide plain of fertile foothills and open grassland known as the Vorbergland.

# **The Mountains, Foothills, and Vorbergland**

The highlands of the Reikland are broken by peaks, crags, and ridges, many topped by ruined castles and watchtowers from bygone eras. Two ranges rise higher than the rest — the Hägercrybs and the Skaag Hills. Further south, the foothills of the Vorbergland lie between the Reikwald forest and the dangerous Grey Mountains.

### **The Grey Mountains**

The forbidding peaks of the Grey Mountains are all but impassable, a near impenetrable border between the Reikland and the Bretonnian duchies. They soar high on the horizon, a constant reminder to Reiklanders of the limits of their land to the west. While there are several dwarf holds here, the Grey Mountains have no true masters and their valleys, plateaus, and slopes harbour all manner of wild creatures, ruins, and mysteries.

Only two reliable passes pierce the frozen heights: the welldefended Axe Bite Pass, guarded by the fortresses of Helmgart and Montfort, and the winding Grey Lady Pass leading from Ubersreik to Parravon. Both passes are guarded by patrols and fortified toll booths. Some impoverished merchants and smugglers take the risk of hiring mountain guides to lead them through lesser passes such as the Crooked Corridor or Durak Way. Many are never seen again.

Countless mines riddle the craggy edges of the Grey Mountains, excavated by order of lords and merchants. Lone watchtowers and stone forts guard these remote settlements against threats from the mountains, many standing amid the rubble of older fortifications that long ago failed in their purpose.

The Skullsmasherz tribe are a mountain-dwelling orc rabble who menace Axe Bite Pass. Under the brutal leadership of Ugrok Craktoof, they range across the mountains from Helmgart to Bretonnia, where Duke d'Montfort is planning to make overtures to Helmgart for an alliance with Margrave von Mackensen against the orcs.

#### **DWARF HOLDS OF THE GREY MOUNTAINS**

Many thousands of years ago, the depredations of orcs and goblins forced several clans of dwarfs to abandon their holds in the Worlds Edge Mountains. They journeyed west and eventually settled within the peaks and valleys of the Grey Mountains, hoping to find riches there. The mountains yielded relatively little mineral wealth, but nevertheless, they built holds and began to trade with the primitive humans who dwelled in the foothills.

The Grey Mountain dwarfs are less wealthy than their eastern brethren and their holds are less impressive, yet to human eyes they are marvels of stonework and engineering. While Karak Norn in the south is the preeminent hold in the Grey Mountains, on the borders of the Reikland there are several smaller settlements and two notable holds:

Karak Azgaraz (Hold of the Fearless Axe) is a relatively young hold, situated close to its trading partner, the city of Ubersreik. The hold's king, Thuringar Zaladrinsson, is considered young and intrepid, launching many expeditions against skaven and night goblins in the hold's underdeep.

Karak Ziflin (Windswept Hold) lies to the north of Axe Bite Pass, closer to Bretonnia than the Reikland. It stands within and atop a broad plateau, buffeted by constant winds from the north. Unusually for a dwarf hold, the Upper Deep Halls protrude upwards from the surface of the plateau. King Rorek Granitehand has little love for humans of any kind, but he tolerates Reiklanders as more trustworthy than the Bretonnians to the west.

The dwarfs of the Grey Mountains have not had to suffer the great losses endured by those who remained in the Worlds Edge Mountains, but they are in decline, and no longer have the numbers to maintain the tunnels and outposts they built long ago. Those places are now overrun with orcs and goblins, stone trolls, skaven, and worse. These ancient halls and byways attract the foolhardy and desperate, who delve into the depths in hope of lost dwarf riches.

# **The Hägercrybs**

Dominating the interior of the Reikland, the mist-shrouded Hägercrybs extend from the Princedom of Altdorf in the north to the Duchy of Ubersreik in the south. Dense forest covers the hills and no road penetrates their heights from east to west.

Antiquarians claim the Hägercrybs were the sacred burial ground of the Unberogens, Sigmar's own tribe. Indeed, ancient cairns are found on many of the highlands, some marked by looming menhirs. It is no coincidence that the Hägercrybs have a reputation for being haunted, and only the brave or reckless are willing to stray into its forests. Those who return tell of thick fogs rising from ancient barrows, accompanied by eerie moans echoing from within, supposedly the spirits of ancient kings hungry for the blood of the living.

Ignoring such talk, landowners with holdings in the Hägercrybs order mines sunk into the hills in search of rare metals and minerals. Most fail, their miners never seen again, but a handful succeed and have brought considerable wealth from the earth.

#### **SECRETS OF THE DEFFWEAVE TRIBE**

Hidden amongst the trees on the steepest slopes of the Hägercrybs are the lairs of the Deffweave tribe, a small tribe of extremely vicious goblins. They string up their prey from trees to emulate the spiders they worship. Should a traveller see wild animals and dead miners suspended in the tree canopy above their heads, they *really* ought to turn back.

When the Knights of the Fiery Heart announce a new effort to exterminate the goblins, they beseech anyone able to swing a sword to join them. The wily goblins are not unprepared, and plan to lead any adventurers who encounter them into the lairs of beastmen who also haunt the hills.

# **The Skaag Hills**

The rocky Skaag Hills lie to the west of the River Bögen and run along the southern bank of the Reik before falling beneath the trees of the Duchy of Gorland. In the heart of the range, the forest recedes from the stony crags to reveal layers of stratified rock rising to the scree-laden highlands of the Prie Ridge. A single road crosses the gentler slopes of the Skaag Hills to the south, starting at Trosreut in the shadow of Castle Grauenberg and wending its way through to Holthausen, but many minor tracks and trails also cross the region, leading to abandoned mines and settlements which once produced silver and iron before the seams ran out. Today, outlaws, hunters, and mutants make use of the buildings that remain.

### **The Vorbergland**

The Vorbergland is a hilly region extending the length of the Reikland between the Grey Mountains and the edge of the province's forests. It is known for its fertile valleys, rolling grasslands, and windswept plains. The most heavily cultivated regions of the Reikland are the southeastern Vorbergland provinces around Böhrn, Ubersreik, Stimmigen, and Dunkelberg. Flourishing towns, villages, farms, and vineyards pepper the verdant valleys and pastures.

Further west, the Vorbergland is tormented by frequent orc and goblin attacks from the mountains, leaving it little more than a sparsely populated hunting ground for wild animals and monsters from the peaks. This land is popular with game hunters and natural historians from the Imperial Zoo seeking to capture rare creatures, though only the unwise travel this region without a heavy guard and knowledgeable guides.

# **THE CENTREPIECE**

Brandt Brechdt is on the trail of a manticore, or so he claims. He has received a handsome advance from Graf Ostarperht von Haal, who wishes to see the creature killed, stuffed, and displayed as the stunning centrepiece of his vast new dining hall. As time goes by, Brandt is beginning to regret boasting that he knew where to find, and how to kill, such a creature. As the hunt dragged on without sign of the quarry, his underpaid henchmen found more gainful employment. Now Brandt has heard fresh rumours of a manticore hunting in the Vorbergland, and is looking to hire cheap help, such as a band of adventurers, to help him track down the beast and make the kill.

# **Drachenberg**

Soaring high above central Vorbergland, the twisting peak of the Drachenberg is visible for miles around. The mountain has a fell reputation and whenever trouble arises, the nearby townsfolk of Wheburg cast an eye towards the mountain and make the comet sign of Sigmar to ward off evil.

The mountain has long been a favoured haunt of the dragons, as well as basilisks, wyverns, and manticores. The immense red dragon, Caledair, the 'Scythe of Fire', once made her lair in caves near the peak of the mountain, scouring the Vorbergland when rage or hunger brought her forth. She has not been seen for more than a century, though none can say for certain if she has migrated to parts unknown or sleeps within the mountain.

# **The Grim, Dark Forests**

The widespread forests of the Reikland do not have the terrifying reputation of the Drakwald or the Forest of Shadows, but away from the busy rivers and settlements, uncharted miles of towering oak, pine, elm, and beech hide much more than desperate bandits and wild animals.

# **The Reikwald**

Other than the Vorbergland, the Reikwald blankets almost the entire Reikland, broken only by rivers, roads, and scattered settlements. The waterways offer safer passage than the uncertain roads, which wind through the favoured haunts of outcasts, cut-throats, and mutants. The roadwardens cannot patrol in sufficient force to keep the byways safe, so upturned coaches and other signs of banditry are a common sight.

Outlaw bands claim open clearings and abandoned ruins, where they lie in wait to ambush the unwary or attack farms and villages. The deepest woods harbour sizeable beastman warherds, who could pour forth from the trees at any time to raid, despoil, and murder. Regiments of the Reikland State Army make occasional forays to clear these isolated places of the beasts within, though it is impossible to penetrate every corner of the forest.

The deepest woods are shadowed and gloomy. Heavy fogs are common, especially along the marshier stretches of the Reik. Scholars claim that the most ancient human tribes ranged across this misty forest. Concentric rings of carved standing stones called Ogham stones remain from a time long before Sigmar, though many are overgrown and almost impossible to find without a guide. Magisters from the Colleges of Magic postulate terrible battles were once fought for these ancient, megalithic sites, and it is not uncommon to find wizards funding explorations into the Reikwald's depths to learn more about the magical stones and their properties.

# **Bloodpine Woods**

The wood of the bloodpine tree is a deep maroon colour and prized by artisans for crafting exquisite furniture, which commands high prices in Altdorf, Marienburg, and Nuln. The Bloodpine Woods are an outcrop of these trees growing down the southern slopes of the Amber Hills. The lumber is difficult to source due to the Spiderclaw tribe, goblins who ride on swiftmoving giant spiders, led by an ambitious shaman, Raknik Spiderclaw. Many a woodcutter has met a grisly fate as food for the monstrous arachnids and their masters. As bloodpine prices increase, merchants are willing to pay mercenaries and others to protect their supply of valuable timber.

# **THE GROVE OF RHULITHRI**

Where the Reikwald covers the Amber Hills, the elves of the Laurelorn forest have established a permanent camp by order of Queen Marrisith. Amongst a grove of ash and oak, they have trained the boughs of trees to form halls and lodges for any of their people who find themselves in the Reikland feeling wary or weary of human company.

Dathior Brightshade established the settlement several generations ago and now oversees Rhulithri on behalf of her queen. The proximity to Altdorf and the emperor's throne is intentional, most of the elves are wise enough to realise that the fate of the Laurelorn is tied to that of the human realm.

While outsiders are not welcome, humans and elves from Ulthuan and Athel Loren are tolerated if they show due respect to their hosts. In recent times, a high elf noble, Princess Kaele of Tor Dynal, has been visiting the vale. Queen Marrisith's subjects consider her cold and distant, but the Cothiqui princess insists that her hosts allow her to see the encampment.

#### **The Grissenwald**

The trees of the Grissenwald grow close together, with distorted trunks and twisted undergrowth. In the darkest woods skulk beastmen, witches, and tribes of feral mutants, who prowl the borders for victims. Most of the local woodsmen travel in groups and seldom stay outdoors come nightfall, and it is commonplace to find fluttering bills posted on roadside trees, offering rewards for the retrieval of family members and friends thought to be lost in the bowels of the forest.

Deep in the interior is a ring of towering monoliths known as the High Circle, where woodsmen believe witches and hedgefolk make pacts with the beastmen.

# **KINBANDS OF TOR SARATHLUI**

Asrai are rare outside Athel Loren and shun the company of other species. However, some asrai are 'lost kindreds', wood elves who settled outside the Loren Forest and maintain their hinterglades in other lands. The Reikwald has one notable asrai settlement, Tor Sarathlui.

Spellsingers have wielded cunning magics to conceal these glades from all but the wood elves. The kinbands here number no more than 150 elves, dwelling around a wooden tower overgrown with vines. This emerges from a stand of wythel trees, planted here many generations ago.

# **The Rivers, Canals, and Lakes**

The Reikland's wealth and economy is largely dependent on the many rivers spilling from glacial lakes and waterfalls in the Grey Mountains, which run through the province to join the mighty Reik. Barges brimful with merchandise navigate the largest of these rivers, shipping goods throughout the Empire or downstream to the great markets of Marienburg.

The richest noble and merchant houses have invested in extensive canals to allow goods move faster than before. The free flow of trade is always under threat, however, from wreckers and river pirates. The roadwardens, guards, and riverwardens are too overstretched to protect every barge, and a good many are no better than the criminals they supposedly pursue.

#### **GRANDFATHER REIK**

Grandfather Reik is a minor god, the patron of the river itself. He is usually depicted as a naked, elderly man with an immensely long beard, entangled with eels and other fish. Also known as the Reikgott, he has no defined cult and no dedicated worshippers as such. Rather, all who depend on the Reik in some way pay respect to Grandfather Reik. They offer small sacrifices and prayers of gratitude at his shrines on islands and the banks of the river.

The priests of Manann, the jealous sea god, maintain that Grandfather Reik is either an aspect of Manann or one of his divine children. Yet many in the Reikland will never see the sea and prefer their own river god to a distant briny deity.

# **The Reik**

The Reik is the mightiest river in the Old World, rising in the Black Mountains before winding through the Empire to meet the Sea of Claws at Marienburg. The many tributaries which flow into the Reik drain the Reikland and much of the western Empire beyond. It is a vital river for the land's prosperity; the waters of the Reik carry more traffic than the rest of the Empire's rivers combined.

North of Nuln, the Reik carves a broad valley through the Reikland, with increasing numbers of riverside settlements as it flows towards the Wasteland. Its span is so broad that it cannot be bridged downstream from Nuln until it reaches Altdorf, where it joins the Talabec and divides into a complex tracework of channels. These spread to form the Altdorf Flats, as the main channel flows through Altdorf, still wide, but narrow enough for the city's five Reik bridges. The capital's preeminence as a trade centre is due, in part, to the fact it is the only place for hundreds of miles where land traffic can cross the Reik.

# **THE LORLAY**

The Lorlay is an imposing rock formation rising from the middle of the Reik, some 40 miles downstream of Grissenwald. The channels on either side of the jutting island run swift and deep, posing a hazard to boatmen unfamiliar with their treacherous cross-currents. Romantic legends tell of a beautiful maiden, pale of skin and supple of limb, who swims these waters at dawn and dusk. Tales of the singing water maiden are especially common among the Reikland's sailors, and if they are to be believed, half claim to have seen her frolicking in the river**.**

# **The Bögen**

The people of Bögenhafen thank Bögenauer, their river's patron deity, for their prosperity. The river's clear, placid waters flow gently enough to allow trade barges to sail upriver nearly as easily as the current takes them downstream.

The Bögen's source lies high in the freezing peaks of the Grey Mountains, warming as it flows through the Vorbergland hills. In the evenings, tendrils of roiling fog rise from its waters, wrapping about nearby settlements, penetrating the Reikwald and obscuring the deeds of those who would rather stay hidden.

#### **The Teufel**

The Teufel flows down from the mountains to Ubersreik, then northwards through Auerswald and Grünburg to the Reik. Iron deposits in the silt lend it a ruddy hue, though storytellers say this comes from the unending wars between the Grey Mountain dwarfs and goblins, which permanently stain the waters blood red. The Teufel is prone to flooding, especially in spring, when its waters inundate the river's forested banks.

# **The Canals**

Over several centuries labourers have excavated canals to link the Reik and its many tributaries, making trade and travel easier and safer for those who use the waterways.

**The Grünberg Canal** — commissioned by Emperor Luitpold III and completed in 2506 IC, this canal bypasses the treacherous shallows of the Reiker Marshes and carries most of the Teufel's barge traffic to Altdorf. A tollhouse at Grünburg extracts a river tax based on vessel length. At the north end of the canal is a lock keeper's house on the outskirts of Prieze. This doubles as a barracks and stables for the twelve roadwardens who patrol the canal's towpath.

**The Vorbergland Canals** — five canals which carry trade from Wissenland across the Vorbergland and beyond. The canal system links Nuln to Carroburg, avoiding Altdorf 's high river taxes. The dwarfs of Karak Azgaraz have recently demanded the canals close, claiming the former archduke of Upper Teufel was violating several treaties by commissioning such poor workmanship from Altdorf 's Imperial School of Engineers. The engineers are deeply offended, as their steam locks and clever water pumps are state of the art for humans.

**The Weissbruck Canal** — this canal carries steady traffic to and from Altdorf, connecting the Bögen and the Reik, while bypassing Carroburg's excisemen. Local bargees whisper strange stories about the canal. While excavating the waterway, dwarf engineers supposedly unearthed prehistoric artefacts which predated even the Grey Mountain dwarf holds. Things have never been right in the area since and some swear that when the moon Morrsleib is full, one can sail the canal north to somewhere other than the familiar waters of the Reik.

# **The Cursed and Fetid Marshlands**

Although Middenland claims many famous marshlands, such as the Furdienst, Midden Marshes, and Shadensumpf, the river banks of the Reikland are no less waterlogged, commonly giving way to fens, mires, and dangerous mud plains.

#### **Grootscher Marsh**

The largest wetland in the Reikland, the Grootscher Marsh spreads from both banks of the Reik in the west of the province. It extends some fifty miles into the Reikland and Middenland from the border with the Wasteland. Many consider it a cursed place, for it is the site of one of the Empire's most famous military defeats of the last century: the Battle of Grootscher Marsh, where the armies of the merchant princes of the Wasteland secured independence from Imperial rule.

The foul-smelling fenland is populated by flocks of ill-sounding birds and infested with river trolls. In lean years when food is scarce, the trolls grow hungry and slip into the Reik to pluck the unwary from passing ships. Periodically, regiments of the Reikland State Army are sent to clear out the Grootscher Marsh, one of the worst punishment details imaginable, and even the hardiest soldiers fear for their lives.

Travellers on the Reik occasionally report the sound of strange, ominous horns echoing through thick fog that gathers swiftly across the marsh. Stories of ancient swamp daemons may be more than mere legend, for riverfolk claim shadowy, shambling figures can be glimpsed moving through the mist.

# **SHROOM BOOM**

Physicians, apothecaries, and scholars all pay high prices for the multitude of rare mushrooms and unusual plants that grow in the perilous Cursed Marsh about the city of Marienburg. In recent times, spores of daemon's tand, rood puffball, and dodeshors polypore have drifted east and sprouted in the murk of the Grootscher Marsh. These fungi are especially valuable and, sensing heavy profits, the merchant Klaes Adaans is hiring mushroom-pickers to set out from Oberseert to forage on his behalf. Fungi hunters have attracted hungry river trolls, as well as bands of swamp-dwelling goblins, hoping to capture the trolls and gorge themselves on mushrooms. Klaes keeps this information to himself; he just wants a steady crop of lucrative fungus. Those with stern hearts and keen eyes can reap real rewards here, or become meat for a troll's belly.

#### **The Altdorf Flats**

The rivers Reik and Talabec split into hundreds of shifting channels, black-soiled mudflats, and marshes that spread north and south of Altdorf. These wetlands are choked with rushes and reeds, with several causeways winding outwards from the city, each one busy with coaches, merchant trains, and heavily patrolled by roadwardens. Stone bridges carry these roads over ditches and mires, including fine dwarf constructions dating back to the time of Sigmar.

Within the Altdorf Flats is a marsh known as the Brackenwalsch, a place of mystery for the Unberogen tribe which called the Reikland home many thousands of years ago. Their kings sought counsel with the Thrice-Cursed Crone of the Brackenwalsch, an ancient crone who gave them cryptic portents of the future.

#### **Reiker Marshes**

Between the towns of Prieze and Babenborn on the Reik, the sluggish confluence with the Teufel spreads across the low-lying land in a myriad of reedy channels. The Reiker Marshes are notoriously treacherous, and inexperienced captains frequently run aground in their deceptively shallow waters. Tattered flags and corroded signposts mark the most perilous sections, but these are woefully insufficient. River guides known as huffers are available to guide boats through the waters for a reasonable price, but wise barge captains avoid the marshes completely, preferring to pay tolls to pass through the Grünberg Canal rather than risk their crew and cargo.

# **THE FOULHERD**

Deep in the Reiker Marshes, a few miles north of the Reik, stands a rotting copse of dead trees. These maggot-ridden trunks once grew from a hillock part-submerged in the mire, and now play host to the Foulherd, a warband of beastmen in thrall to the Chaos God Nurgle. The braylord is a beastman champion of Chaos known as Bilehoof, who skulks amongst his pestigor brethren spreading infestations of parasites and imploring them to give up their flesh to the Plague Lord. Bilehoof seeks to build a towering pile of carcasses amongst the dead trees, so that they will rot in the fetid air and incubate daemonic maggots gifted to him by Nurgle.

# **Uhland Bogs**

The Westerfluss river is the natural border between the Wasteland and the Reikland, which cuts through a windswept peatland known as the Uhland Bogs. Villagers living nearby venture into the mossy bogs to cut and dry peat, which they sell along the Reik for fuel. In the southwest depths of the bog, a number of ancient, carved stones rise from the sodden land, drawing local cultists of Rhya and older deities to worship during important equinoxes.

# **THE CROWSTONES**

At the heart of a black bog is a circle called the Crowstones, which have a particularly foul reputation. Locals warn never to travel to the stones near the festival days of Geheimnistag or Hexenstag, for they claim crows gather in impossible numbers and unspeakable things rise from the bog to wreak terror upon the living.

# **THE POWERS THAT BE**

Reikland is arguably the most powerful of the Empire's provinces; it is the seat of the emperor and a major centre of military and trading might. However, governance anywhere in the Empire is confusing and even more so in the grand province, where the various Imperial, noble, mercantile, and religious authorities vie for control.

## **Ruling the Empire The Emperor**

In 2512 IC, the Reikland is the heart of the Empire, so to understand its politics, one must start with the emperor, His Imperial Majesty Karl Franz Holswig-Schliestein. In theory he commands absolute control over the Empire, through issuing edicts, setting taxes, and raising armies. In practice, his power is checked (or frustrated, depending on your view) by other political bodies, numerous exceptions, and special privileges.

The emperor is also the grand prince of the Reikland and prince of Altdorf. Around Karl Franz are his privy council, composed of confidantes, companions, ambassadors, and advisors. While they have no formal role, they help him govern by providing counsel and helping him hone his arguments in private.

The emperor employs imperial plenipotentiaries as special representatives to courts and freistadts across the Empire. Each is chosen by Karl Franz himself, although their competence varies greatly. For example, Herr Gustav Leutze in Salzenmund is a canny diplomat, building allegiance with the ambitious Theoderic Gausser of Nordland and his allies in the coastal towns. In contrast, Lord Mattias von Emden, who is flagrantly ambitious and half-witted, was posted to Liecheburg in Stirland to keep him as far away from the emperor's court as possible.

# **The Electoral Council and Prime Estates**

The emperor was elected by the Electoral Council, a body of the Empire's most significant leaders: the electors. Each of these is a ruler in their own right, mostly elector counts with their own grand provinces, but also representatives of the cults of Sigmar and Ulric. There are currently 15 electors, who meet infrequently in Altdorf 's Volkshalle to debate matters of great import. Electoral status has changed hands many times as borders shift, noble families rise and fall, and provinces lose or gain the right to select a new emperor.

Emperors are chosen from heirs nominated by each elector, with much politicking and settling of scores. The election of Karl Franz in 2502 IC was especially fraught, and the young prince of Altdorf conducted some impressive diplomacy to become emperor over Graf Boris Todbringer of Middenheim.

The Prime Estates is a body of representatives of the electors who meet in Altdorf to scrutinise the emperor's edicts and decide whether they are in the interest of the Empire. In other words, do they support their provincial ruler's ambitions? They can check bad decisions by the emperor, but also delay or effectively veto important matters, such as military campaigns.

# **The Council of State**

The emperor appoints this body to oversee the running of the court and provide guidance on affairs of importance to the Empire. Members represent military, religious, fiscal, legal, and magical matters, as well as foreign relations. Several of the councillors sit on other governing bodies: the grand theogonist, the supreme law lord, and the high marshal are notable examples. The Council of State has no constitutional power, but as they control access to the emperor and choose what information to share with him (or not), few decisions are made without their support.

# **Ruling the Reikland**

The grand prince of the Reikland, currently Karl Franz, rules the province by right of birth. His land is divided into a patchwork of individual fiefs governed by dukes, counts, margraves, high priests, abbots, and other rulers. These provinces, whose lords swear directly to the grand prince, are collectively titled the Reikland Estates. Most have been ruled by the same families or institutions for uncounted generations, affording them significant influence in local politics.

# **The Reikland Council**

As grand prince of the Reikland, Karl Franz appoints a council to run the province on his behalf. Just as the Council of State oversees matters relating to the Empire, the Reikland Council governs the day-to-day running of the grand province. The council's ten high lords are drawn from the great families of the Reikland on the basis of political expedience, merit, and dynastic compromise.

The council meets weekly in the Wilhelm Chamber of the Volkshalle in Altdorf. It is rare for more than six high lords to attend simultaneously, and almost unheard of for Karl Franz to attend in person. The various personalities on the council have their own ambitions and agendas, which do not always align with one another, or indeed with the intentions of the grand prince of the Reikland.

# **The Reikland Diet**

The Reikland Diet is an assembly of the heads of the province's noble households that scrutinises and ratifies any decree made by the grand prince of the Reikland. The crown retains certain powers, however, including the right to summon and command the state army of the Reikland, the raising of emergency taxes in times of crisis, the right to authorise new coinage, and the right to call a High Lord Steward's Court should a powerful noble happen to face a criminal charge.

The diet meets as required in the Holzkrug Chamber of the Volkshalle in Altdorf, to either pass or return decrees to the crown with suggested amendments. As most lords of the Reikland Estates do not have time to attend the diet themselves, they generally send favoured children, spouses, relatives, or minions in their place. If an important decree is under scrutiny, worried lords from across the Reikland will attend in person.

This can be a source of friction, as several noble families of the Reikland have histories of feuding and one-upmanship. Nobles in the Empire guard their grievances as jealously as dwarfs, and there have been occasions when meetings at the Volkshalle have degenerated into belligerent barracking and even duelling.

Members of the diet are proud and do not like to feel that the grand prince is dictating the law without their due consideration. Until recently, Karl Franz had been an effective operator and the diet waved through most decrees without objection. Lately, the Emperor caused significant consternation when he issued a decree declaring Graf Sigismund von Jungfreud of Ubersreik to be a traitor. This declaration was announced after midnight to an almost empty chamber, thus securing support for Altdorf 's invasion of the Duchy of Ubersreik.

# **The Reikland Estates**

Vassals of the grand prince rule the Reikland Estates. They can do as they will with their lands, parcelling out fiefs to family and friends as they wish, though the creation of new hereditary titles requires the approval of the prince of Reikland and the Reikland Diet. Each estate has a range of distinct legal and military obligations to the crown, but all must build and maintain at least one regiment for the Reikland State Army,

> soldiers typically used in peacetime as watchmen, roadwardens, or guards. Most Reikland Estates are feudal, comprised of smaller fiefs ruled by vassals, many of whom hold hereditary titles that have existed for centuries.

#### **THE DUCHY OF WALLENSTEIN**

The Duchy of Wallenstein is the ancient demesne of the von Wallenstein family. It is a hilly region, sloping down from the rolling hills of the Hägercrybs to the sodden loam of the Teufel, Ober, and Tranig river valleys.

Graf Ferdinand von Wallenstein rules the duchy. He is a man of no obvious talent and a prodigious appetite for good cheer and ale. While he carouses in Nuln and Altdorf, his uncle, Lord Adelbert von Wallenstein, oversees the duchy from the family manor outside Auerswald. The Wallensteins come from an ancient line of proud warriors, dating back before the Age of Three Emperors. The Wallensteins have dynastic connections to several prominent Reikland families, not least House Holzkrug; the High Lord Ambassador is a relative and ally of the family.

Within the duchy lies the freistadt of Auerswald, whose charter from the emperor grants the town council authority over the town and its environs. The graf's lawyers, however, maintain that the rivers themselves lie beyond the freistadt's jurisdiction, allowing the noble rulers to levy taxes on all river traffic.

The duchy is wealthy, thanks to trade through Auerswald and the mineral riches beneath the Hägercrybs. Rumours across the Reikland claim the Hahnbrandt silver mine attracted the attention of the von Jungfreuds of Ubersreik, who maintain a claim to the duchy's southern and western territories. According to dramatic accusations in the Reikland Diet, these claims were merely a pretext for the Ubersreik graf's planned invasion of Wallenstein. There is little love lost between the von Jungfreuds and the von Wallensteins, whose feud stretches back centuries, but the only evidence presented by the Wallensteins amounts to hearsay and a purloined letter of dubious origin.

When Emperor Karl Franz declared Graf Sigismund von Jungfreud a traitor and sent several regiments from Altdorf to occupy Ubersreik, Graf Ferdinand and Lord Adelbert were careful to keep their opinions on the matter to themselves. Rumours cover the gamut of possibilities, some suggesting that Graf Ferdinand had a hand in the downfall of the Jungfreuds, while others suggest his dismay at Karl Franz's rash overreach is leading to a rapid reconciliation between the squabbling aristocrats.

#### **THE GRAF'S CHIEF VASSALS**

Graf Ferdinand von Wallenstein has several vassals who each hold lands under his rule. These families hold their tenure at the sufferance of the von Wallensteins, and in return for his continued favour they are conspicuous in their eagerness to support him politically and militarily.

**Baroness Eliza von Behmen of Hahnbrandt** — the baroness occupies territory along the Hahnbrandt Valley, location of the graf's most valuable mine. Every few weeks, a consignment of silver and iron wends its way from Hahnbrandt to Auerswald. While heavily guarded, it is a tempting target for ambitious thieves and outlaws.

**Warden Kerrod Machen of Hochwallenstein** — oversees the northwest highlands, including the Hägercrybs and Lorvehn ranges within the duchy's borders. Much of this region is haunted high moorland, scattered with pre-Unberogen barrows, the rumoured homes of restless spirits.

**Burgrave Otto Skarlingmann of Castle Wallen** — a valued retainer of the ruling family and youthful companion of Adelbert von Wallenstein, the burgrave is castellan of the Castle Wallen and its extensive estates. Like his friend Lord Adelbert he is a keen huntsman, and the castle's walls are bedecked with the stuffed heads of beastmen.

**Baron Thymo Tiedeman, Marshall of the Oberwalds** — a hereditary position, the Marshall of the Oberwalds holds the lands and settlements in the east of the duchy. He is a boorishly patriotic man who has been known to administer arbitrary rough justice to those he finds churlish.

**Lady Ilse von Steche, Warden of Stromlund** — Stromlund is the bailiwick south of the Teufel and west of the Tranig. Lady Ilse is a tough character who eyes the von Jungfreuds with suspicion. People in Auerswald say her lands bear a curse, pointing to the constant inclement weather around Stromdorf as evidence of this.

Aside from these noble lords, two other figures loom large in the politics of the region, though their loyalty to the von Wallensteins is less certain.

**Abbot Bors Binder, Provost of Sigmar's Seat Monastery** — the monastery's lands spread southeast of the Hägercrybs. The brethren are known for their prodigious appetites, both for battle and good beef. Seeing as this land belongs to the cult of Sigmar, the provost does not owe Graf Ferdinand any fealty and has raised concerns in the past about the frictions between the Wallensteins and the Jungfreuds.

**Bürgermeister Halmo Eigern of Auerswald** — a fractious character, Eigern oversees the freistadt of Auerswald. While he meets with Graf Ferdinand regularly and treats him with good grace, he is deeply resentful of the nobility, and privately agitates to eliminate all influence of the Wallensteins on the town.

#### **Other Powers in the Reikland The Cult of Sigmar**

Sigmar was born and raised in the heart of the Reikland and his cult dominates the province, which is home to both the grand theogonist and the Cathedral of Sigmar in Altdorf. As head of the cult and one of the Empire's most powerful political figures, the grand theogonist's authority often overshadows that of other faiths, leading to tensions between the cults.

The cult has considerable secular power in the Reikland, with holdings across the province and numerous templar chapterhouses. The Knights of the Fiery Heart and Knights Griffon own land in the northern and southern Reikland respectively, although neither are as powerful as the secular Reiksguard and Knights Panther.

A more sinister force is the Holy Order of the Templars of Sigmar, also known as the witch hunters. An accusation or insinuation of corruption is a powerful weapon against even the nobility. It is also not unknown for ambitious witch hunters to exert their influence for personal gain.

Priests and abbots of Sigmar are important political figures in the towns and cities of the Reikland. The cult's temples can raise tithes, and monasteries possess estates with tenants who must pay tribute to their landlord. While there are pious Sigmarites amongst the priesthood, there are others whose greed, personal aspirations, or comfort take precedence over any religious duties.

# **THE LONG ARM OF THE LAW**

The Empire's laws are a patchwork of local customs, noble privilege, religious authority, and imperial decree. Theft, murder, and the broad offence of causing mischief are illegal almost everywhere, though punishments vary from fines to execution. In the Reikland, unlicensed magic is among the gravest crimes, and witch hunters enjoy broad powers.

Status heavily influences justice. Gold Status Characters often receive greater leniency, while those of lower Status can expect far harsher punishments for the same crime. A thief might merely be beaten by the watch for robbing a peasant, flogged for waylaying a merchant, but risks imprisonment or even execution for stealing from a noble.

Defending yourself in court usually requires an opposed Charm or Lore (Law) Test, with the Difficulty modified by the evidence, the circumstances, and the accused's Status.

Minor punishments should rarely interrupt an adventure. For more serious crimes, the GM should usually provide an opportunity to escape, though habitual offenders can expect the Empire's justice to catch up with them eventually.

#### **The City of Altdorf**

As the largest city in the Empire, Altdorf is very much a power in its own right. While it is the capital of the Reikland, it tends to look to its own interests over those of the rest of the province. The Council of Altdorf rules the city, an ever-changing, illdefined body of councillors, which meets in the Volkshalle to discuss an ever-changing, ill-defined range of matters. The citizens of Altdorf are proud of the Reikland, although they have a slightly condescending view of Reiklanders elsewhere and resent paying taxes to support their provincial neighbours.

#### **The Freistadts**

Over the centuries, some towns and cities in the Reikland have become free towns through the granting of charters by the grand prince or the emperor (when they were not the same person). These are centres of commerce, ruled by a council of guildsmen and merchants. There are tensions between nobles who fear loss of influence (and tax revenue) and the upcoming burgomeisters who profit by their independence.

The Reikland was foremost amongst the establishment of freistadts and there are several of note in the province. Kemperbad, Bögenhafen, and Auerswald are the largest, each with their own wider hinterland. Smaller examples include Essel, Kalegan, and Gerlbach.

#### **The Guilds**

The Reikland has a flourishing urban population and lively mercantile culture, so the dominant political force in most towns are the guilds. These represent the interests of professions and skilled workers, as well as those who profit by selling what they produce. Guildmasters of the craft, labour, and merchant guilds sit on town councils that determine local governance, although their power varies greatly, even within the Reikland. #### **Village Councils**

Smaller settlements have informal gatherings for the leading villagers to determine matters on behalf of their peers. This is a practice that dates back to the first tribal councils. Each village is led by a village elder or headman, who represents the villagers to the lord's bailiff and any other officials. The council is responsible for resolving local disputes, mustering the militia, and petitioning the bailiff or lord on behalf of the village.

#### **The Military**

The Reikland has a powerful military, and the armed forces have influence over the running of the province. The generals of the state armies are invariably nobles whose martial responsibilities considerably increase their political standing, such as General von Dabernick, who commands the Altdorf regiments that are currently occupying the town of Ubersreik.

There are several state armies in the Reikland. The army of Altdorf recruits and billets in the capital, particularly in the military-dominated district of Hammerpfad. It is wellresourced and proud, frequently sent to campaign far from the city. The light buff uniforms and eagle standards of the Reikland State Army are a common sight throughout the province and beyond. Several nobles maintain their own armed forces, such as Graf Ostarperht von Haal's Army of Eilhart and the von Jungfreuds' Ubersreik Regiments.

The Reikland Fleet of the Imperial Navy is also important, insofar as it protects shipping on the Reik. That said, Sea Lord von Hopfberg's admirals lament their apparent lack of influence in Reikland politics. This is not helped by the fact that the upper echelons of the fleet are riddled with nepotism and snobbery.

# TIMELINE OF THE REIKLAND

Being a summary of the major events concerning the history of the Grand Province of Reikland.

#### **c. -500 IC**

The Unberogen tribe of humans settle the land where Altdorf now lies, and begin fortifying the area. It will be sacked many times by rival tribes, beastmen, orcs, goblins, and other vile creatures. Nevertheless, the site is strategically important at the confluence of the Reik and Talabec, and a mixture of conquest and trade helps the settlement flourish. Soon, scholars claim, the fortified town is known as the 'rich village', or Reichsdorf. As centuries pass, this becomes Reikdorf, with the surrounding land called the Reikland.

#### **-30 IC**

A twin-tailed comet streaks through the sky, heralding the birth of Sigmar to Chief Björn of the Unberogen tribe in Reikdorf. The comet leads a crazed warparty of orcs to Sigmar's birthplace, and his mother, Griselda, is killed in the attack, leaving Sigmar with a life-long hatred of Greenskins.

# **-15 IC**

A merchant-train from Karaz-a-Karak is ambushed by orcs, and they capture King Kurgan Ironbeard. Sigmar rescues the king, and in return is named a dawonger, dwarffriend, and given the king's greatest heirloom, the magical warhammer Ghal-Maraz.

#### **-8 IC**

After his father dies, Sigmar becomes chief of the Unberogen tribe.

# **-7 IC**

Sigmar realises the threat posed by orcs and goblins is far too much for his tribe to tackle alone, so he starts a campaign to bring all the surrounding human tribes under his rule.

#### **-2 IC**

After several years of warfare and diplomacy, Sigmar binds twelve of the human tribes under his rule and allies himself with several more.

#### **-1 IC**

The First Battle for Black Fire Pass. The largest horde of orcs and goblins the world has seen is defeated by humans and dwarfs, led by High Chief Sigmar and King Kurgan, ending the centuries-long Goblin Wars.

#### **0 IC**

Sigmar is crowned emperor over the chiefs of the twelve tribes and the Empire is born. The dwarf runesmith Alaric the Mad is commissioned to create twelve runeswords, one for each of the tribal chiefs, as a symbol of their office and as thanks for their sacrifices to end the Goblin Wars.

# **1 IC**

The First War Against Chaos. The newly founded Empire is attacked by the forces of Morkar the Uniter, Everchosen of Chaos. The war is desperate, short, and devastating. Eventually, Morkar is slain by Sigmar in a day-long battle that was said to have split the earth and rent the sky.

#### **c. 2 IC**

Sigmar names the twelve tribal chiefs his 'counts'. Modern scholars believe the title derives from the Classical word *comes*, meaning 'companion', for they fought beside him against the orcs and goblins, and later against the Chaos tribes of the north.

#### **c. 7 IC**

Sigmar kills the necromancer Morath and takes the Crown of Sorcery. Recognising its evil, Sigmar locks it away beneath Reikdorf.

#### **11 IC**

Battle of Drakenmoor. A great and terrible necromancer leads an army of goblins and daemons against Sigmar's new capital and suffers the first defeat of his eternal existence. This shocking loss will haunt him long after he reincorporates several centuries later.

#### **15 IC**

Battle of the River Reik. Nagash, the Lord of Undeath, gathers a shambling horde of unliving monsters, and attempts to claim the Crown of Sorcery from Sigmar. The undead almost overwhelm the Reiklanders and their dwarf allies, but, after an exhausting battle, Sigmar eventually strikes Nagash down, causing the unliving army to crumble to dust.

#### **50 IC**

After five decades of extraordinary rule, Sigmar vanishes. To avoid destroying Sigmar's empire, the tribal chiefs eventually agree to vote for a new emperor from their own number, and select Siegrich of the Asoborn tribe. Thus, the counts became 'elector counts', and each swears to be a companion and protector of their elected emperor.

#### **51 IC**

Emperor Siegrich I dies in a hunting accident. After a fraught month, Prince Hedrich of the Unberogens is elected Emperor Hedrich I, bringing the seat of the Empire back to Reikdorf.

# **69 IC**

Johann Helstrum arrives in Reikdorf and claims to have holy visions concerning Sigmar. He preaches that he witnessed Sigmar's ascension to godhood. Such is the love the Unberogens have for their lost emperor, Helstrum is readily believed.

#### **73 IC**

Johann Helstrum builds a temple to Sigmar in Reikdorf. History will forever remember him as the first grand theogonist of the Cult of Sigmar, and the first man to recognise Sigmar's divinity.

#### **c. 100 IC**

Emperor Hedrich I receives the twelve runeblades from Alaric the Mad that were commissioned a hundred years earlier. Each of these 'Runefangs' is a unique weapon of extraordinary power, and they are passed to the tribal chiefs. In time, the Runefangs will become powerful symbols of the elector counts.

#### **246 IC**

A High Temple to Sigmar is completed in Reikdorf, and it acts as the centre of worship for a cult that now extends throughout the Empire. This rapid expansion leads to open conflict with the cults of Ulric and Taal.

#### **990 IC**

Emperor Ludwig I, commonly called Ludwig the Fat, grants the grand theogonist a vote to select the next emperor. Though some elector counts cry foul, and the other cults are astounded at the blatant favouritism, Emperor Ludwig appears far more interested in the lavish banquets prepared for him by the Cult of Sigmar in Reikdorf.

#### **1000 IC**

A thousand years after Sigmar was crowned emperor, the Cult of Sigmar completes a new High Temple in Reikdorf. It is the largest temple in all the Empire, and cements Sigmar worship as the primary cult in the Reikland. In celebration, Emperor Ludwig I renames his city Altdorf, demonstrating its age and importance to the Empire as a whole.

### **1053–1115 IC**

The Rule of Boris Goldgather. Emperor Boris I proves to be exceptionally unpopular and corrupt, with his rule known for exorbitant taxes, weak leadership, and a complete neglect of the military.

#### **1106–1110 IC**

Rise of the Drakwald. Beastmen and other vile creatures swarm from beneath the twisted bowers of the Drakwald, sacking villages, towns, and fortifications across the Drakwald province. When the last heir to the Drakwald throne is slain in battle with an enormous bestigor, Emperor Boris I chooses to place the Drakwald Runefang in his Altdorf palace. Publicly, when a suitable heir is found, the Runefang will be passed on. Privately, Boris has no intention of ever giving up the magical blade.

#### **1111–1115 IC**

The Black Plague. Disease sweeps through the Reikland, killing nine in every ten people, and debilitating half of those left alive. skaven then erupt from their under-Empire and attack. When Emperor Boris I dies of the plague in 1115 IC, no-one replaces him in the ensuing anarchy.

#### **1115–1124 IC**

The Rat Wars. Skaven move to systematically enslave the remaining population of the Empire, and effectively wipe out what remains of the Drakwald province. In response, Elector Count Mandred of Middenland rallies a desperate defence, and with the help of elven allies from the Laurelorn Forest, finally drives the skaven back below ground. At the end of the war, the victorious Mandred is elected emperor by the three surviving elector bloodlines.

#### **1152–1359 IC**

Age of Wars. Emperor Mandred II is assassinated by the skaven in 1152 IC. The elector counts cannot agree on a successor as they are too concerned about rivals invading their devastated grand provinces, so a fraught interregnum ensues for over two centuries. During this time, the princes of the Reikland rule their Grand Province unfettered as civil war rages across the Empire.

#### **1359–1547 IC**

The Time of Two Emperors. In a desperate bid to end the bloodshed, the Electors meet in Altdorf and eventually agree on a choice for emperor, crowning Elector Count Wilhelm of Stirland as emperor by a majority of one. Elector Countess Ottila of Talabecland is outraged at this, and claims the vote is rigged. When she returns to Talabheim she declares herself empress without a vote, and is supported in her claim by the Cults of Taal and Ulric. She then outlaws the Cult of Sigmar in Talabecland, a decree that stands for almost a thousand years. The civil wars escalate.

#### **1421 IC**

Shipbuilding rises to prominence in Altdorf's natural harbour, the Reiksport. Soon, quality vessels built in the Reikland are plying the Reik river system.

#### **1489 IC**

The prince of the Reikland formally commissions a navy, worried about the influence of ships sailing from Talabheim, Carroburg, Nuln, and Marienburg. To help limit their influence, Altdorf builds several low bridges across the Reik and Talabec to block larger ships and to control trade.

#### **1547–1979 IC**

The Time of Three Emperors. After Sigmarites botch an assassination attempt when electors refuse to back their choice for the next emperor, the elector count of Middenland denounces the imperial elections as a corrupt sham, and declares himself the rightful emperor. He has the support of the cult of Ulric, which had recently fallen out with the Talabecland emperors.

The Empire now has three emperors: The Electoral Emperor supported by the cult of Sigmar, the Ottilian Emperor supported by the Cult of Taal, and the Wolf Emperor supported by the Cult of Ulric. The civil wars intensify.

#### **c. 1450–1550 IC**

Knights returning rich from the crusades against Araby found new knightly orders and chapterhouses across the Reikland. They also fund the first temple of Myrmidia in Altdorf, a simple building sponsored by the newly invested Knights of the Blazing Sun.

#### **1681 IC**

The Night of the Restless Dead. The dead stir in the Gardens of Morr. Corpses rise, bones rattle, and the dead walk the land, sowing terror and confusion. Entire towns and villages are overrun before dawn brings a reprieve from the rapacious hunger of the deceased.

#### **1707–1712 IC**

WAAAGH! Gorbad! and the First Siege of Altdorf. Taking advantage of the divided Empire, the orc warboss Gorbad Ironclaw invades with a tide of orcs and goblins at his back, destroying the Grand Province of Solland, sacking Nuln and much of Wissenland, and eventually sweeping through the Reikland, razing almost a third of its settlements. His green wave eventually breaks against the walls of Altdorf; but not before the prince of Altdorf, and electoral emperor, Sigismund IV is killed.

# **1979–2303 IC**

The Dark Ages. Elector Countess Magritta of Westerland is elected empress, but the Cult of Sigmar refuses to crown her, or indeed any other elector count or countess, leaving the electoral emperors without a voted emperor. The entire electoral system collapses, and soon most provinces fight only for themselves. Petty warlords claim titles on whims and new kingdoms, counties, duchies, princedoms and more rise and fall as terror spreads across the former Empire.

#### **2010–2146 IC**

The Vampire Wars. The Empire has collapsed into thousands of competing factions, and the vmpire lords of Sylvania rise to exploit the turmoil. In total, three major wars result as three different vampire counts attempt to eradicate the fractured Empire. Each time, the vampires are driven back through a mixture of unlikely alliances, desperate gambits, and clever strategy.

#### **2051 IC**

Second Siege of Altdorf. The vampire lord Vlad von Carstein is slain. His wife, Isabella von Carstein, commits suicide, unable to continue unlife without him. Their armies soon splinter into feuding factions, ending the First Vampire Wars.

After the inconclusive Battle of Four Armies ended in multiple assassination attempts by supposed allies on the Empire side, it is decided that it is time to elect an emperor to pull everyone together. Elector Count Helmut is the most popular candidate for this, until it is discovered he is actually a zombie in the thrall of Konrad von Carstein, the very vampire lord they are trying to defeat. All plans to elect an emperor are abandoned.

### **2135 IC**

Third Siege of Altdorf. The vampire lord Mannfred von Carstein launches a surprise winter attack on the Reikland capital as it recuperates from a summer of civil conflicts, but is eventually driven back when the grand theogonist of Sigmar uses a forbidden spell to unbind the vampire's necromancy.

#### **2203 IC**

A rift into the Realms of Chaos opens in the Grey Mountains resulting in the annihilation of almost every living soul from Bögenhafen to Ubersreik. After more than a week of terror, the rift mysteriously closes.

The Great War Against Chaos. Asavar Kul, Everchosen of Chaos, leads a horde of unimaginable size into Kislev, laying waste to everything in his path. In response, Magnus von Bildhofen, a young Nulner noble claiming to be inspired by Sigmar, rallies the fractured and broken Empire into a mighty army to relieve Kislev. He eventually defeats Asavar Kul at the Gates of Kislev alongside Kislevite, dwarf, and elven allies.

The Reign of Magnus the Pious. Magnus von Bildhofen is elected Emperor Magnus I, the first elected emperor representing all Sigmar's Grand Provinces for almost a thousand years. Riding the wave of popularity he secured by winning the Great War Against Chaos, he immediately initiates sweeping reforms to end the corruption that previously brought the Empire to its knees, creating new laws to limit the nobility, cults, guilds, and much more. He also oversees the foundation of many new institutions, including the formal creation of the Empire State Armies, Imperial Navy, and, controversially, the Colleges of Magic in Altdorf, making magic legal across all the Empire for the first time.

### **2308–2310 IC**

The Third Parravon War. The Bretonnian duchy of Parravon invades the Reikland across the Grey Lady Pass, claiming the military escalation in Ubersreik caused by the mustering of the new State Army breaks an ancient treaty. They are quickly driven back, and the following year Parravon is besieged. Eventually, after a year of occasional skirmishes outside Parravon's extraordinarily high walls, and a great deal of standing around shouting insults, the king of Bretonnia treats on behalf of Parravon with Emperor Magnus I, ending the war.

The Fourth Parravon War. The Bretonnian duchy of Parravon again invades the Reikland across the Grey Lady Pass. Ubersreik is besieged twice during the war, but doesn't fall. The Night of a Thousand Arcane Duels. War erupts between the Eight Colleges of Magic in Altdorf, razing large sections of the city and resulting in the death of six of the eight Patriarchs. The Cult of Sigmar pressures the Prince of Altdorf and Emperor Dieter IV to lock the Colleges down, which they do, resulting in the execution of many wizards, and effectively bringing legal magic use to an end in the Empire. Many of the surviving wizards flee the Colleges, never to return.

#### **2420–2424 IC**

WAAAGH! Grom! The goblin warboss Grom the Paunch leads an enormous horde of goblins across the Empire, sacking many Reikland towns and villages, before eventually heading westwards, undefeated, where he then takes to the sea. The lack of wizards from the suspended Colleges of Magic is widely blamed for the repeated military defeats suffered by the Reikland's armies.

#### **2429 IC**

Westerland buys its independence from the Empire by bribing Emperor Dieter IV, reforming itself as the Wasteland with Marienburg as its capital. Using anticorruption laws put in place by Magnus the Pious almost a hundred years earlier, the elector counts depose Dieter in the ensuing scandal. He is replaced by Grand Prince Wilhelm of House Holswig-Schliestein of the Reikland, who is named Emperor Wilhelm III, starting the imperial dynasty that rules the Empire to this day.

The Battle for Grootscher Marsh. Emperor Wilhelm III, under pressure from the elector counts to respond to the Wasteland's secession from the Empire, gathers the State Armies to invade Marienburg. In late autumn, the opposing forces meet at the Grootscher Marsh just outside Siert, and the Empire is routed by the advanced Marienburg navy, well-trained mercenaries and militias, and the magics of the Wasteland's high elven allies. Begrudgingly, Wilhelm verbally recognises the Wasteland's independence, but refuses to ratify it with a treaty. Marienburg accepts this and draws their new border at Siert.

#### **2430 IC**

Appalled at the State Army's inability to effectively counteract the elven mages and Marienburg ships, Emperor Wilhelm III reinstates the Colleges of Magic after their 15-year suspension and invests significant resources into shipbuilding at the Reiksport.

#### **2431 IC**

The Great Fire of Altdorf. The newly reinstated Bright College accidentally sets Altdorf alight when a spell misfires, burning many buildings to the ground. Many lobby Emperor Wilhelm III hard to suspend the Colleges again, but eventually he decides to keep them open, but with greater oversight from the Cult of Sigmar.

#### **2441 IC**

The Marriage of Queen Thurma and King Brokk. Queen Thurma of Karaz-a-Karak travels to Karak Norn to marry King Brokk Ironpick. Many dignitaries from the Reikland attend their nuptials, and partake in much feasting and drinking. Her relatives were underwhelmed by her match. He may be a king, but in the eyes of Worlds Edge Mountain Dwarfs, Karak Norn is a petty marcher realm.

#### **2453 IC**

Fourth Siege of Altdorf. The Liche King, Arkhan the Black, invades the Reikland with a seemingly endless swarm of walking corpses that march straight for Altdorf. Once the siege begins, Arkhan slips unnoticed into the High Temple of Sigmar and steals the dreaded Liber Mortis kept within. Minutes after he escapes, his army collapses, leaving thousands of carcasses mouldering outside the city walls as state soldiers look on in confusion and relief.

#### **2483 IC**

Emperor Luitpold III signs treaties with the Wasteland to allow warships of the Imperial Navy to pass through the city-port of Marienburg. For the first time since the secession of the Wasteland, the Reiklander navy sails the high seas, although the tolls to do so are extortionate.

#### **2502 IC**

Emperor Luitpold III dies in his sleep. In a close vote, Luitpold's son is elected to replace him, and is crowned Emperor Karl Franz I in the High Temple of Sigmar in Altdorf.

# **SETTLEMENTS**

The Reikland is one of the most populated and civilised of the Empire's grand provinces. Nevertheless, great swathes of the realm are still wild, blanketed in forest and untouched by human hand. Even after 2,500 years of expansion, most Reikland settlements are found along the Reik and its many tributaries, reached by river if not always by road. Only in the southern breadbasket are rural settlements found spread across the landscape, connected by roads and canals.

# **Altdorf and the Manifold Towns**

It is said that all roads and rivers lead to Altdorf, the largest, richest city of the Empire, but there are many other interesting places to visit beyond that city's high, white walls. Numerous towns spread across the Reikland, including several freistadts, which strive to keep noble rule limited only to matters of war.

#### **Altdorf**

His Imperial Highness Emperor Karl Franz I of House Holswig-Schliestein, Elector Count, Grand Prince of the Reikland, Prince of Altdorf, Count of the West March, and Sigmar's Chosen, holds court in Altdorf, the capital of the Reikland and the whole Empire. It stands tall at the confluence of the mighty Reik and Talabec rivers, where many major trade routes converge. Little wonder it is the wealthiest city in the Empire. Every day, fresh, wide-eyed hopefuls arrive in the city, making for a cosmopolitan, rowdy populous.

Merchants, nobles, and diplomats from across the Old World and beyond mingle with folk of every rank, species, and creed. Elves, dwarfs, and even wizards are common sights, rarely attracting more than a wary glance or muttered prayer. Such a vast and varied population gives Altdorf an energy found nowhere else in the Empire, but also one of its most infamous characteristics: the Great Reek. During the height of summer, the stench of sewage and stagnant water grows so overpowering that many wealthy citizens retreat to their country estates.

Each morning, mist rolls in from the Flats to shroud the city before gradually lifting with the sun. When the fog lingers, tempers often fray. Altdorf 's citizens are easily whipped into a mob by the city's many agitators, and riots can erupt at the slightest provocation, real or imagined. Whatever the cause, the crown is always swift to crush unrest before it spreads.

#### **THE IMPERIAL CALENDAR**

Two moons cross the night sky. Mannslieb, the larger, governs the tides and is sacred to Manann, completing its cycle every 25 days. The smaller Morrslieb is an ill-omened moon, believed to have been forged by Morr from the wreckage of a daemonic invasion, and few willingly travel beneath its light.

The Imperial year lasts 400 days, divided into twelve months of 32 or 33 days and six unnumbered festival days. Four mark the solstices and equinoxes, while the other two — Geheimnisnacht (Night of Mystery) and Hexensnacht (Witching Night) — occur when both moons are full and are widely feared.

The months are Nachhexen (After Witching), Jahrdrung (Year-Turn), Pflugzeit (Plough Tide), Sigmarzeit (Sigmar-Tide), Sommerzeit (Summertide), Vorgeheim (Before Mystery), Nachgeheim (After Mystery), Erntezeit (Harvest-Tide), Brauzeit (Brewmonth), Kaldezeit (Chillmonth), Ulriczeit (Ulric-Tide), Vorhexen (Before Witching).

There are eight days in the Imperial week. These are Wellentag (Workday), Aubentag (Levyday), Marktag (Marketday), Backertag (Bakeday), Bezahltag (Taxday), Konistag (Kingday), Angestag (Startweek), and Festag (Holiday). The origin of these names is lost to history, and now they carry little meaning. Markets are often open on days other than Marktags, and holidays vary according to the deities of local temples.

#### **Auerswald**

The lively free town of Auerswald stands by the confluences of the Teufel, Tranig, and Ober rivers, one of the busiest and best protected trade routes in the Reikland. The folk of Auerswald tend to be strong-willed and determined, but their easy charm and famous wit ensure that no matter how steely their negotiations, they rarely cause offence.

The bürgermeisters of the freistadt have taken to building vulgar townhouses to prove their success. Carved woodwork and elaborate gargoyles cover the buildings, drawing envious comments from rival guildsmen. Much of Auerswald is built on thick stilts over the floodplain of the river Teufel, which is often in spate. Connected by a bewildering labyrinth of ramps, bridges, and rope ladders, outsiders can easily become lost, especially when worse for wear.

Rumours abound that there is an organised gang of blackmailers and confidence tricksters operating from the town, though no witnesses have lived long enough to testify to their existence.

#### **Bögenhafen**

Bögenhafen is the largest, most prosperous town in the Vorbergland and serves as the crossroads of the region. It is a crucial stop on the principle trade route between the Empire and Bretonnia, lying equidistant between Altdorf and Montfort.

Bögenhafen is largely governed by its town council, with the local nobility content to let them run the place so long as the taxes keep flowing. Members of the Merchants Guild dominate the council, along with representatives from the Stevedores and Teamsters Guild.

#### **Diesdorf**

Diesdorf is a distinctive sight for travellers on the Reik, as almost every roof is thatched, testament to the town's reliance on the trade in wheat, most of which is shipped downriver to Altdorf. Visitors may conclude, however, that its chief export is religious fervour.

A little over 200 years ago, Magnus the Pious gave a legendarily powerful speech in the town. Since that day, Diesdorf has become a major pilgrimage site for devout Sigmarites. On holy days, the town's population doubles, with the numerous temples and shrines to Sigmar full of worshippers in prayer. Most families in the town have at least one member serving in the cult's priesthood.

#### **Dunkelberg**

The southern market town of Dunkelberg stretches across several hills along the banks of the River Grissen. The older, richer portions of the town sit atop the hills behind high stone walls, giving the wealthiest townsfolk a commanding view of the surrounding countryside and a sense of security. The poor districts are more vulnerable to goblin warbands, who occasionally strike out from their lairs in the Grey Mountains and the depths of the wild Graugrissen Forest.

Despite its size, Dunkelberg has a rustic air, with local farmers selling livestock and produce from markets lining the winding streets. Outside the town walls, on cloudless days, the Mercers Guild pay farmers to lay out linen between crops in so-called bleachfields, where the sun dyes the fabric white.

#### **Eilhart**

The vineyards around Eilhart produce succulent grapes, the source of a wine widely considered one of the Reikland's best whites. It is renowned for its crisp, light flavour, and the famously mild hangovers it leaves behind. The town also produces sharp, acidic beers which make use of fragrant hops and local grain.

Given the excellent quality of its alcoholic beverages, Eilhart has become a popular destination for epicures seeking to sample its wares from the many drinking houses, breweries, and vineyards.

#### **Grünburg**

Grünburg's boatwrights craft high quality barges, found plying their trade along the whole length of the Reik. The Boatwrights Guild is one of the most powerful on the town council, with many of the other guilds subservient to its dictates.

The open land to the southeast of Grünburg is known as the Battle Plains. Long ago, these verdant fields witnessed the Battle of Grünburg, where the fearsome orc warlord Gorbad Ironclaw was countered in a clash of mounted troops. The Battle Plains now attract interest from antiquarians and grave robbers, all keen to excavate debris from the blood-soaked earth.

#### **Holthusen**

Nestled along the River Schilder, Holthusen is a major point on the trade route between Eilhart and Marienburg. The coopers of Holthusen make particularly sturdy barrels, most famously the Holthusen Hogshead, which is so tough that one can be used as a butt for a whole day's worth of archery practice without spilling a single drop. Most vintners and brewers in the western Vorbergland use Holthusen barrels, and many age their stock in cool, subterranean warehouses beneath the town.

Holthusen nestles behind several rings of tall palisades, as wild beastmen from the nearby Reikwald regularly attack without warning. Folk believe the beasts have an insatiable thirst for alcohol, so the town has taken to leaving barrels of cheap wine at the forest's edge to placate the beastmen. Should any witch hunters hear of this appeasement, there may be a reckoning.

#### **Kemperbad**

An ancient town with a long and convoluted history, the Grand Freistadt of Kemperbad stands atop a 500-foot cliff above the Reik and River Stir. Though part of the Reikland, Kemperbad lies on the eastern bank of the Reik; historically the town has been fought over, and ruled by, nobles from Talabecland, Stirland, and the Reikland, changing hands time and again through a series of marriages, treaties, and wars. In 1066 IC it gained a charter from Emperor Boris, and since that day, Kemberbad has been a fiercely independent freistadt, celebrated for making the finest brandy in the Reikland.

Thanks to the town's prodigious wealth, the wealthy of Kemperbad are legendarily ostentatious, dressing in outfits so gaudy as to shame a peacock. Unsurprisingly, the neighbouring nobility regard Kemperbad's swollen coffers with envious eyes, and would dearly like to destabilise the town.

#### **Schädelheim**

Schädelheim sits beside the Grootscher Marsh, where the River Mos meets the Reik. Its position on the busy trade route between Marienburg and Altdorf has made it prosperous, and the town boasts a large number of inns and berths for passing ships and barges. Ferries here regularly run travellers back and forth over both rivers, and it is a hub for the disparate communities of the nearby marshlands.

Just south of town stands an ancient temple dedicated to Morr. The grand, crumbling building has presided over Schädelheim since before its founding, surrounded by a vast garden of black roses and tombstones. Many of the locals have an especially strong affinity with Morr, which does not sit easily with some Reiklanders, who believe Sigmar should be paramount.

#### **Schilderheim**

Standing resolute at the confluence of the Schilder and Reik rivers, Schilderheim is one of the Reikland's most important trading towns. It is also home to a surprisingly diverse array of wildfowl, particularly wading birds. Most notable is the red crane, a sedentary river bird found predominantly on the Reik, known for using heavy stones to crack open freshwater clams.

The Stevedores Guild has recently raised its prices in response to the volume of trade passing through the town. To avoid extra costs, several merchants have taken to conducting their business on the river itself, swapping barges to avoid loading and unloading. Some of these merchant houses have found their wharves set alight, warehouses raided, and barges sunk. The stevedores deny everything, so the Merchants Guild will pay handsomely for proof they are responsible.

#### **Stimmigen**

Stimmigen is a busy market town in the Suden Vorbergland, with a bridge over the River Ober on the route to Ubersreik. Its lush orchards produce the Reikland's best known apple, the sweet, yet tart, Ernwald. The Ernwald only flourishes hereabouts and its cultivation has attracted a significant halfling population, whose delicious apple pies, pastries, and crumbles, not to mention crisp cider, can be found in local hostelries all year round, and especially during Pie Week at the start of autumn. All the folk of Stimmigen celebrate this halfling festival, as an opportunity for rich and poor alike to enjoy tastings and eating competitions, and generally gorge themselves on all manner of sweet, succulent, baked delicacies.

#### **Ubersreik**

The town of Ubersreik stands near Grey Lady Pass, one of only two major passes over the Grey Mountains into Bretonnia. Because of this, the town regularly sees traders and travellers from across the Old World. It has recently seen upheaval as the ruling von Jungfreuds were declared traitor by Emperor Karl Franz, and abandoned the town to seclude themselves at their ancestral seat in the Duchy of Black Rock to the west.

Ubersreik has a long association with the nearby dwarf clans of the Grey Mountains. Unusually, there are members of the city's dwarf population who sit upon the town council. The elder folk designed and built one of Ubersreik's more famous edifices, its spectacular bridge over the River Teufel. Widely regarded as one of the most impressive feats of engineering to be found in the Reikland outside of Altdorf, it connects the trade road which runs from Bögenhafen to Dunkelberg and then on to Nuln. Ubersreik deals in trade of all kinds, but is most wellknown for ore and the skilled metalwork of the dwarfs.

#### **IMPERIAL DWARFS IN THE REIKLAND**

The old alliance between the Empire and dwarfs has existed since Sigmar's time and nowhere is it stronger than the Reikland. There have been times of persecution, but thanks to the preeminence of Sigmar's cult, many dwarf clans have settled alongside humans in towns along the Reik and in the hills. These tight-knit communities usually take traditional dwarfen trades in fine craft and construction, although some adopt more human roles (and vices) which their hidebound mountain kin would reject.

Highland and mining towns such as Hahnbrandt and Delfgruber typically have larger dwarf populations than agricultural settlements in the Vorbergland. The major cities and towns such as Altdorf, Dunkelburg, and Schilderheim have dwarf quarters where the resident clans hold sway.

#### **Weissbruck**

In the past century, Weissbruck has grown rapidly into a bustling port for goods and travellers moving between Bögenhafen and Altdorf. Several generations ago, the ruling Gruber family made a shrewd decision to mine the rich deposits of coal and iron found in the Skaag Hills. The revenue enabled them to commission dwarf engineers to construct a canal in 2462 IC, which brought wealth and new folk to the town.

Weissbruck retains the character of a typical mining town, yet the mines' output has diminished. More and more miners have descended from the Skaag Hills — a growing, agitated, unemployed workforce who have little to do but drink cheap rotgut and pick fights with strangers.

#### **Wheburg**

Wheburg is the first town encountered after leaving the Grey Mountains on the road from Helmgart to Bögenhafen. Mountain patrols pass through regularly, and troops are always billeted in the town's sizeable barracks. They have taken to displaying their banners in the streets, and walking through Wheburg, one can see the colours of numerous Reikland regiments.

The town is a welcome respite for travellers who have survived the mountains' extreme weather and the threat of orcs, goblins, or other monsters. Wheburg has a reputation for hospitality, and its locals are regarded as the friendliest in the Reikland, for a price. A visitor can buy almost any pleasure in the rowdy and licentious taverns, inns, and gambling dens lining the streets. Spending time in Wheburg prior to marriage has become something of a rite of passage for well-heeled Reiklanders.

# **The Bastions and Fortresses**

Many strongholds defend the Reikland, built to protect the province from invasion and its nobles from plague and disquiet amongst the commoners. In the south, bastions in the Grey Mountains defend against raiding goblins, trolls, and the restless dead, not to mention the neighbouring kingdom of Bretonnia. Along the Reik, castles from the Age of Three Emperors once defended the borders from other grand provinces. Today, many are little more than relics, their crumbling stonework lost to time. Others remain, a constant reminder to ambitious nobles in Middenland, Talabecland, Stirland, and Wissenland that the Reikland stands resolute.

It perches precariously on the edge of steep cliffs, far too tight for wagons or horses; the slightest slip of a foot could lead to a painfully inglorious end. It is completely impassable in the winter.

The Crooked Corridor was once known only to goat herders and smugglers, until a spate of goblin raids in 2454 IC brought the pass to the attention of the Emperor Mattheus II. He decreed the construction of a fortress here to watch for invasion, and so his masons raised Blackstone Tower from the local dark stone and slate to stand sentinel over the gorge.

#### **Helmgart**

Amongst the troops garrisoning the fortress are a number of storied regiments.

# **The Villages, Hamlets, and Holy Places**

Most of the Reikland's villages and hamlets cluster close to towns for protection and trade. In the Vorbergland, settlements are strewn more freely amongst the farmlands, dotting the landscape every few miles or so, relying on fortress garrisons and regiments from the towns for protection.

The Reikland is home to an uncounted number of isolated monasteries, abbeys, way-temples, and other holy sites. Many stand in the most unlikely places, whether for religious or historical reasons, from mountainsides and the depths of forests to islands in the middle of lonely lakes.

#### **Monastery of the Holy Word**

Deep in the Reikwald west of Altdorf, far from any easily traversed road, stands the Monastery of the Holy Word. This hallowed site is tended by the Sigmarite Order of the Anvil, the monks responsible for preserving the religious laws and practices of the Cult of Sigmar.

The Monastery of the Holy Word houses the order's greatest treasure: the *Testaments of Sigmar*. After he departed the Empire, Sigmar's retainers recorded the accounts of those who knew him as a man. Historians and priests consider the *Testaments* the most accurate record of Sigmar's words and deeds, so it is little wonder that they are some of the holiest texts in the Empire.

The Monastery of the Holy Word will admit penitents and scholars who seek obscure details about the foundations of Sigmar's law. Many travel from across the Empire to query the black-and-green robed monks on doctrine or as part of a pilgrimage. None outside the order may consult, or even touch, the original documents, but they may gaze upon a few of the original pages if they can prove indisputable piety.

#### **Rottfurt**

Scholars and wizards throughout the Empire speak of Rottfurt in hushed, reverential tones. Though primarily a village of shepherds, trading wool and mutton, Rottfurt also produces a famous, and famously pricey, sheepskin parchment, Rottfurt Silver. The parchment possesses a faint sheen, takes ink well, and resists fading far longer than other, lesser parchments.

The thick-wooled, pale sheep of Rottfurt are the village's pride and livelihood. The shepherds indulge them, letting them feed on the lush grass of the Hammastrat Heights and wander freely during the day. The shepherds are both herders and militia, tasked with protecting their precious flocks at all costs.

#### **SLEEPING SHEPHERDS**

Of late, the sheep have been going missing. Despite their vigilance, those on guard have found themselves falling asleep. On waking, another sheep has vanished. The locals are angry and distraught, with many wild and spectacular theories on the cause of this woolly calamity.

The culprit is a local witch, Traudl Fluchschmied, who employs magic in order to lull shepherds to sleep while she steals their sheep. She is using the animals in an attempt to win the trust of a monster whose lair she has discovered in the nearby woods. If she is apprehended, she may be able to call this beast to defend her.

#### **Wörlin**

The fishing hamlet of Wörlin is almost impossible to spot from the river. The settlement is surrounded by a number of small, rocky islets and promontories, and sheltered by thousands of bowing trees. These Willows of Wörlin line the banks of the Reik for many miles around, and it seems none have any interest in using their lumber. A great deal of trade passes by Wörlin, but very little actually happens at its wharf, as most passing goods are bound for more lucrative markets.

#### **THE ROOT OF THE PROBLEM**

On the festival day of Sonnstill, the council of the Wörlin's elders gather to 'Water the Willows'. This simple ceremony involves singing, dancing, feasting, and the slicing of a virile stranger's throat, splashing blood across the thirsty roots of the Queen Willow, quenching her sanguinary appetites for one more year.

Once sated, the Queen is pleased, and will ensure the hamlet's safety from the children of the forest. Should anything interrupt the ceremony, the Queen awakens and summons her children, bringing a horde of braying beastmen to slaughter all in their path.

#### **Zahnstadt**

Isolated Zahnstadt is considered by locals to be the last village of the Vorbergland, after which the rolling hills turn barren and cold before reaching upwards to the Grey Mountains. In the shadow of those peaks, Zahnstadt skulks along the southern bank of the River Mos, and is nestled deep in a dark valley with bleak crags rising to all sides.

Even during the long days of summer, the sun only shines on the village briefly, hiding behind the surrounding cliffs in the morning, and creeping behind the mountains as the day lengthens. In the winter, there is barely any direct sunlight at all, locking the village in darkness. Despite this, the folk of Zahnstadt are welcoming. Its homely inn, the *Wayward Sun*, is famous for its ever-burning hearthfire and the upbeat songs that echo into the night.

# **The Ancient Sites and Terrible Ruins**

Prehistoric monuments and relics of ancient religion litter the Reikland, places of mystery and the forgotten folk who raised them. Most towns and villages boast their own local ruins, with accompanying eerie tales of haunting and darkness.

#### **The Darkstone Ring**

The path to the Darkstone Ring lies north of Blutroch, near the Altdorf-Bögenhafen road. It leads through woodland to a place of terrible, ancient power. When night falls, evil emanates from the old glade; the six suggestively-carved stones within glow green with a wan luminosity, flaring bright as Morrslieb waxes.

At the centre of the Darkstone Ring stands a monolithic slab of unidentifiable rock, permanently stained with the blood of the countless innocents sacrificed there throughout the millennia. Despite the ring's fell reputation, travellers are lured by legends of mystical potential and lost artefacts of terrible arcane power. Not even frequent sightings of beastmen and cultists, especially around the fated night of Geheimnisnacht, can deter these brave — some may say foolhardy — souls.

#### **Helspire**

Looming perilously in the peaks overlooking Axe Bite Pass, the Helspire is a grim ruin, the shell of an ancient fortress, its stones inlaid with the relics of countless fallen. No one knows who may have built the tower, and it is so remote that no Bretonnian duke or imperial baron would think it worthwhile to repair and garrison the place.

The villagers who live in the lee of the mountains tell strange tales about the citadel. They claim that on rare nights of occult significance, eldritch lights blaze upon its battlements, casting eerie shadows across the pass. At such times, so they say, the Helspire reveals itself as a citadel of unquiet dead, and skeletal knights sally forth to scour the mountainside, terrorising both Reiklander and Bretonnian alike.

Treasure hunters sometimes clamber up to the Helspire, daring to confront whatever necromantic horror lurks within its walls. Invariably, they return disappointed, having found nothing within the ruins but dust and old, yellowed bones.
