# • **BESTIARY** •

*'Throughout the forests, mountains, and shadowed reaches of the Old World and lands beyond, creatures of cunning and deadly habits dwell. This volume catalogues their forms, behaviours, and the hazards they pose, so that scholars may study them, the unwary recognise peril before it claims life, and that students may gape at the depictions of their monstrous forms and appendages.'*

> — Dr. Heironymous Blitzen, Professor of Bestiaria, University of Altdorf

Many terrible and wondrous creatures dwell in the wild places of the Empire and beyond its borders. No less deadly are those threats originating in the cities, towns, and villages of those we tend to assume are our friends, countrymen, or allies. This bestiary gives you game information for a range of opponents, associates, or passers-by the Characters might encounter in the course of their adventures.

# **Customising Creatures**

The information presented is for the basic example of each particular individual. You can adapt these entries for variety, or to create more powerful creatures, by changing their Characteristics and adding Skills, Talents, and Creature Traits. The quickest way to create fast NPC adversaries is to use Creature Traits; all creatures come with one or more Creature Traits as standard, but additional ones can be added as required. The listed Optional Traits for a creature present common variants of the species, but you can apply any Trait to any creature if it fits your game. See page 356 for Creature Traits.

The templates on page 353 are a quick way of creating a variety of opponents from more organised species or societies. Apply the template to the basic information for the species to create a Soldier, Skirmisher, Elite, Leader, Commander, Spellcaster, or Spellcaster Lord. You could even use the Career system to develop well rounded and, depending on how much XP you decide to spend on them, terrifyingly powerful allies or antagonists.

#### **CREATURE CHARACTERISTICS**

The Characteristics in the **Bestiary** are average values, rounded to the nearest 0 or 5. To individualise Characteristics, subtract 10 and add 2d10. For example, a Characteristic of 30 becomes 2d10 + 20. You can also use this method to create Player Characters from species normally intended as NPCs by assigning them an appropriate Career — an ogre Soldier, skaven Engineer, or vampire Noble, for example.

# **CREATURE HIT LOCATIONS**

To determine a Hit Location for quadrupeds, arm results are foreleg hits, and leg results are rear leg hits. For birds, arm hits become wing hits. For snakes, a roll of 01–19 hits the head, 20–00 hits the body. For spiders, 01–09 hits the head, 10–79 hits the legs and 80–00 hits the body. For any creature 2 steps larger than you (see Size on page 360), you'll hit the location closest to you (or in line of sight for shooting).

If an animal has a Hit Location without a Critical Table, such as a tentacle, tail, or wing, roll on the Arm table and describe the results in an appropriate way.

# **Creatures and Equipment**

Melee weapons are listed in a creature's attacks showing the weapon's name, its Skill with that weapon and the total Damage it inflicts (the creature's Strength Bonus is included) in parentheses, followed by any Qualities or Flaws in italics. For example, a spear-armed creature would have: **Spear**  (30/+7) *Impale*.

Ranged weapons are listed with the weapon and ammunition it uses, followed by parentheses containing the creature's Skill with that weapon and the total Damage it inflicts (appropriate Strength Bonus is included), its range in yards, and any Qualities or Flaws in italics. For example, a creature armed with a Bow and Arrows would have: **Bow and Arrows** (30/+7) 50 yards, *Impale*.

Many creatures are armed with teeth, claws, horns, or other natural weaponry as their primary weapon. Unless otherwise mentioned, the Reach of such weapons is Personal and they possess no Qualities or Flaws.

Where an *Armour* value is provided, the total Toughness Bonus + Armour Points is shown in parentheses.

*Armour* and *Weapons* given to bipedal creatures with hands are just suggestions. If the GM wishes, such creatures could be armed and armoured with anything from the Consumer Guide, and can be assumed to have any Skills they need to use the items without penalty.

### **BESTIARY FORMAT**

- 0 **Name:** The creature's name.
- 0 The creature's description.
- 0 **Attributes:** The creature's 12 Attributes.
- 0 **Attacks:** A summary of the attacks the creature can usually make. Where numbers appear such as (30/+7), the number before the slash is the Melee or Ranged Skill used to make the attack, and the number after the slash is the damage added after determining if the attack hits. Where a creature does extra damage due to being a particular Size, this is factored in.
- 0 **Armour:** A summary of the creature's defences. Often there are options to add extra items of armour to a creature.
- 0 **Traits:** The Creature Traits the creature almost always has. Skills and Talents may also be listed in the case of more detailed creatures.
- 0 **Optional:** Common Creature Traits the creature may be given when creating your own.

# **Gnome**

An elusive and enigmatic folk, gnomes are isolationist and secretive. Little is known of their numbers, though scholars estimate a few tens of thousands live in the Old World, with perhaps more in the lee of the Worlds Edge Mountains. The largest known community is Glimdwarrow in the Mirror Moors. Home to no more than three thousand gnomes, it is still recovering from the depredations of the goblin warlord Grom the Paunch nearly a century ago.

Most gnomes are acerbic and quick-tempered, standing a little taller than halflings, but with slimmer frames and distinctive bulbous noses. They share many cultural traits with dwarfs: their menfolk cultivate long bushy beards, they are skilled smiths, engineers, and artisans, and they live in networks of burrows beneath hills and mountains. Unlike dwarfs they are known to work magic, though they seem to be limited to working with the Lore of Shadows. They are also rumoured to have a strange affinity with many small animals that live in the woodlands and hillsides near their burrows. It is rare to see a gnome in a human city, though the occasional adventurous individual ventures beyond their hidden homes.

# **Ogre**

Big, loud, violent, and perpetually hungry, ogres hail from the distant east and wander the world in search of new meals. They readily adopt local dress and customs, having learnt that fitting in is more likely to lead to their next meal than frightening everyone away. In the Empire, ogres are best known as mercenaries and hired muscle. Many also find work as labourers, with the Halfling Gaffers Guild having effectively cornered the market in cheap ogre labour — much to the annoyance of its rivals.

### **HALFLINGS AND OGRES**

No one knows why, but halflings and ogres share a curious affinity. Many halfling clan elders employ ogre bodyguards, and it is said the Grand Elder of the Moot rarely travels without his old friend Zorarth Legbiter, a hoary ogre who has lived in the Empire for almost a century. In return, many ogre mercenary bands employ halfling cooks. The arrangement does not always end well, however. Golgfag Maneater, captain of the imaginatively named Golgfag's Maneaters, once employed a small halfling clan to feed his regiment after a great victory. The arrangement worked perfectly — until Golgfag discovered the cooks tasted even better than the meals they prepared.

# **CREATURE TRAITS**

### **Afraid (Target)**

The creature gains Fear of the Target. When encountered, they must make an Extended Psychology Test (page 183). Until their SL equals or exceeds the Target's Fear Rating, the creature cannot gain Momentum, and Melee Attacks made against them by the Target benefit from Advantage. See page 183 for *Fear* rules.

### **Amphibious**

The creature is as at home in water as they are on land. They have the Swim Skill, add +1 SL to all Swim Tests, and move at full Movement through water.

### **Animosity (Target)**

The creature dislikes the Target. When the creature encounters the object of its Animosity, make a Psychology Test (page 183). On a failure, it must attack the target socially or physically, as appropriate, gaining +1 SL on Fellowship based Tests to oppose or disparage them. See page 183 for full *Animosity* rules.

### **Belligerent**

The creature loves to win a fight. As long as they possess Momentum, they are immune to *Fear*.

### **Bestial**

The creature has no rational thought or language. It shies away from fire and gains a *Broken* Condition if struck and wounded by it. In defence, it only uses the *Dodge* Skill. If it loses more than half its Wounds, it will attempt to Flee unless protecting its young or cornered, or unless it has the *Territorial* Trait. If so, it enters *Frenzy* (see page 184).

#### **Bite**

The creature may lose Momentum to make a Free Attack. The Damage equals Rating and includes their Strength Bonus already.

**Free Attack** - Weapon Skill or Ballistic Skill attack that does not use your Action on your Turn.

## **Blessed (Deity)**

The creature can enact Blessings, the relevant deity is indicated in parentheses. A creature with this Trait has a Pray Skill at Fel + 10 (or more at the GM's discretion).

The creature can bounce high, perhaps with powerful limbs, magic, or stubby wings. When Charging, Leaping, or Sprinting, they double their Movement Attribute and can ignore all intervening terrain and creatures as they leap over them.

### **Breath**

The creature's breath is a powerful weapon. As a ranged attack, they can activate their *Breath* as a Free Attack, targeting 1 creature they can see within 20 + Toughness Bonus yards and all creatures within SB yards of that target. The attacker makes an Opposed Ballistic Skill/Dodge Test against all targets (the attacker's single roll Opposed by each individual target). If a creature without a BS makes a Breath Attack, count it as having a BS of 30. Losing targets take Rating Damage. Further, if the Trait has any of the following Types in parentheses, apply the associated rules.

- 0 **Acid:** Inflicts Damage of TB +4. All weapons and armour carried by the targets suffer 1 Damage.
- 0 **Cold:** Inflicts Damage of SB +2. Targets gain a *Fatigued* Condition; until they lose the Condition, their weapons gain the *Slow* Flaw.
- 0 **Electricity:** Inflicts Damage of SB +2. Damage ignores metal armour. Targets gain a *Stunned* Condition.
- 0 **Fire:** Inflicts Damage of SB +3. Targets gain an *Ablaze* Condition.
- 0 **Poison:** Inflicts Damage of TB +2. Targets gain a *Poisoned* Condition.
- 0 **Smoke:** The area fills with smoke, blocking line of sight and causing Suffocation (page 169) for Toughness Bonus Rounds.

The creature is immune to their own *Breath*. This attack is *Magical*.

## **Champion**

The creature is an extraordinarily skilled warrior. If they win an Opposed Test when defending in melee combat, they can cause Damage just as if they were the attacker.

### **Chill Grasp**

The creature's touch chills their enemies' souls. If they lose Momentum, then for their Action, they can attempt an Opposed Weapon Skill/Dodge or Melee Test. If they win, the target loses 1d10 + SL Wounds ignoring Toughness Bonus and Armour Points. This attack is *Magical*.

#### **Bounce** XII **Cold-blooded**

The creature is cold-blooded and slow to react. They gain Advantage to Cool Tests. If they gain the *Surprised* Condition, this Condition is not lost the first time it should be (typically at the end of the Round or if they are attacked).

### **Constrictor**

The creature can squeeze and crush their prey. Any successful hit gives the target an *Entangled* Condition. The creature may then enter a Grapple if they wish (see page 167).

### **Construct**

The creature is a construct of magic, quite mindless, and bound together with enchantments. It has no Intelligence, Willpower, or Fellowship Characteristics, and need never Test them. If it is ever called upon to Test based on Willpower, use Toughness instead. If it has no spellcaster controlling it, or does not possess the *Territorial* Trait, it wanders mindlessly. For the purpose of calculating its Wounds, use its Strength Bonus whenever Willpower Bonus is required. All its attacks are *Magical*.

### **Corrosive Blood**

The creature's blood is corrosive. Every time they are wounded, blood splashes free and all targets Engaged with them take 1d10 Damage (roll once and apply to all targets).

### **Corruption (Strength)**

The creature is tainted by Chaos, or perhaps suffused with Dark Magics. They are a Corrupting Influence with a Strength marked in parentheses. See page 187.

#### **Dark Vision**

The creature can see in the dark as if it were daylight.

### **Daemonic (Rating)**

The creature's essence is raw magic, and unholy ichor pumps through what passes for their veins. Daemonic creatures do not require the normal prerequisites for life such as food, water, air, and so on.

All their attacks are *Magical*. Roll 1d10 after any blow is received, if the creature rolls Rating or higher, the blow is ignored, even if it is a Critical. They may not use this ability to ignore *Magical*  blows such as those caused by creatures with the *Magical* Trait or weapons with the *Magical* Quality. Should the creature be reduced to 0 Wounds, their soul returns to the Realms of Chaos immediately, removing them from play.

### **Disease (Type)**

The creature carries the disease listed, though whether they suffer from the disease is up to the GM. Others will have to Test as appropriate for Contraction (see page 179).

### **Distracting**

The creature distracts or confuses foes, possibly exuding a soporific musk or nauseating reek, or through its bizarre and horrifying appearance. All living targets within a number of yards equal to the creature's Toughness Bonus suffer a -2 SL penalty to all Tests. A target can only suffer this penalty once, no matter how many *Distracting* foes there are.

### **Ethereal**

# **Fear (Rating)**

The creature causes supernatural *Fear* in other creatures. Opponents must make an Extended Psychology Test (page 183). See page 183 for *Fear* rules.

### **Fly (Rating)**

As the creature's Move, they can fly up to Rating yards. When flying, they ignore all intervening terrain, obstacles, or creatures. At the end of the move, they decide whether they have landed or are still flying. They can use this move to Charge. If they start their turn flying, they must choose to Fly for their Move. If they cannot do this, the GM decides how far the creature falls (see page 169).

When targeting them, measure horizontal distance as normal, then increase range by 1 step. So, a Long Range shot would become Extreme Range, and if they were at Extreme Range, they could not be shot at all. Afterwards, they gain a *Fatigued* Condition.

### **Ghostly Howl**

The creature can emit a hideous scream, capable of killing those who hear it. Once per turn, this creature can lose their Momentum to unleash a howl as a Free Attack. ### **Hatred (Target)**

When the creature encounters the Target, they must make a Psychology Test (page 183). On a failure, they become subject to *Hatred*. While *Hatred* lasts, they must attack the hated foe by the fastest and most deadly means possible, gain +1 SL on Melee and Ranged Tests against them, and are immune to *Intimidate* and *Fear* (but not *Terror*) caused by the Target. See page 184 for full *Hatred* rules.

### **Horns**

The creature has horns or some other sharp appendage (if their *Horns* Trait represents a different feature, it will be noted in parentheses). When the creature charges into combat, they may make a Free Attack with their *Horns*, with a Damage of SB +4.

### **Hungry**

### **Immune to Psychology**

### **Immunity (Type)**

### **Many Heads (Number)**

If the creature has the *Bite*, *Horns*, or *Tongue* Traits it can make a number of Free Attacks equal to the number of healthy heads. ### **Size (Various)**

This Trait represents creatures whose size differs from the game standard (i.e. roughly human-sized). There are five steps of Size, ranging from Tiny to Monstrous.

| Size      | Examples                             |
|-----------|--------------------------------------|
| Tiny      | Butterfly, Mouse, Pigeon, Cat, Hawk, |
|           | Human Infant                         |
| Average   | Giant Rat, Halfling, Human Child,    |
|           | Dwarf, Elf, Human                    |
| Large     | Horse, Ogre, Troll                   |
| Enormous  | Griffon, Wyvern, Manticore           |
| Monstrous | Dragon, Giant, Greater Daemon        |

#### **Size Combat Modifiers**

Large creatures do more Damage when they land a hit. These modifiers don't apply to Ranged Attacks or extra attacks. NPC profiles already include these modifiers.

- 0 **Small:** Damage as normal
- 0 **Average:** Damage as normal
- 0 **Large:** Damage as normal plus additional SB Damage
- 0 **Enormous:** Damage as normal plus additional SB Damage
- 0 **Monstrous:** Damage as normal plus additional (2 x SB) Damage

#### **Defending Against Big Creatures**

When fighting a creature of larger size, you suffer a penalty of -2 SL when using *Melee* to defend in an Opposed Test. It is better to dodge a giant swinging a tree trunk than try to parry it!

#### **Fear**

Agressive creatures cause *Fear* in those smaller than them.

#### **Moving in Combat**

Creatures ignore the need to *Disengage* from melee combat with smaller combatants.

#### **Opposed Strength**

During Opposed Strength Tests (and similar), if one creature is 2 or more *Size* steps larger, they win automatically. If one creature is 1 *Size* step larger, the smaller creature must score a Critical to contest the roll. If they do, SL are compared as normal. All other results mean the larger creature wins.

#### **Outnumbering Larger Creatures**

Larger creatures count as several creatures for the sake of working out if they are outnumbered in combat.

- 0 **Small:** Counts as 1 creature
- 0 **Average:** Counts as 1 creature
- 0 **Large:** Counts as 2 creatures
- 0 **Enormous:** Counts as 3 creatures
- 0 **Monstrous:** Counts as 4 creatures

So, you would need 4 *Size (Average)* creatures to outnumber a *Size (Large)* creature by 2 to 1, or 3 *Size (Large)* creatures to outnumber a *Size (Monstrous)* creature by 2 to 1.

Conversely, for the sake of larger opponents being daunting enough, they do not themselves count as multiple opponents when working out if they receive a bonus for outnumbering themselves. 2 *Size (Large)* Ogres outnumber 1 *Size (Average)* Human 2 to 1, not 4 to 1.

# **ADJUSTING A CREATURE'S SIZE**

If you wish to use Size to make a creature bigger then increase *Strength* and *Toughness* by +10 and reduce *Agility* by -5 per step of size you increase the creature. Reverse this if you wish to make a creature smaller.

**Example:** *The Giant Spider described in the Bestiary is Size (Small) with a Strength of 15, Toughness of 25, and an Agility of 35. If it were made Size (Large) it would have a Strength of 35, Toughness of 45, and Agility of 25.*

*Its Wounds would increase to 26, its fangs would inflict +5 Damage and its Bite would increase to +6 Damage.*
