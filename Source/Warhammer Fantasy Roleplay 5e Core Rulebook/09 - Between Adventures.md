# • **BETWEEN ADVENTURES** •

*'Oh, they come by every few weeks, and take that same table there by the fire. A bit battered, maybe, but with coin to spend. I don't believe half the stories they tell about mad orcs and feral goblins, buried dwarfen treasures, and dark gods at the edge of the world. But there's folk who come by just to hear 'em told. So of course I jack up the price of the ale when I see them coming. That's just good business, that.'*

– Leopold Seidel, Proprietor of *The Stuck Pig*

Characters aren't constantly embroiled in plots, intrigues and quests. This chapter helps describe the events between adventures, and the activities Characters can pursue during their downtime.

Activities such as spending XP, changing or advancing your Career, and going on extended shopping trips should ideally be done between sessions so that you can maximise the time spent roleplaying at the table.

#### **ROLEPLAYING DOWNTIME**

The Between Adventures part of a game should be quickly resolved at the end of an adventure, or between the GM and each player individually, before the next adventure begins. Events or Endeavours shouldn't ordinarily turn into small RPG sessions, as this risks over-focusing on the instigating player or derailing the ongoing campaign. If your group loves roleplaying all their Characters' interactions, focus on the key relationships and spend a minute or two on each. Adding more detail to the NPC who fences stolen goods for them, for example, makes the world come alive more vividly, and makes it feel much more personal for the player if something threatens that NPC as part of a subsequent adventure.

# **DURING DOWNTIME**

- 0 Characters recover all Wounds.
- 0 Players may spend XP to purchase Characteristics, Skills, and Talents.
- 0 The GM rolls a Regional Event table to determine what's taking place in the world around the Characters.
- 0 Each player rolls a Character Event to find out what's happening to their Character during Downtime.
- 0 Characters may purchase items and Trappings from shops before they undertake Endeavours.
- 0 Each Character engages in a variety of activities called Endeavours and resolves any matters that arise from their choices.
- 0 Aside from money gained or saved due to Endeavours, all of the Characters' money is spent.
- 0 Characters are then ready to start their next adventure.

Between adventures, you may spend XP to advance your Characteristics and Skills, acquire new Talents, and advance your Career Level. Your Career Level determines what Characteristics, Skills, and Talents are available to you.

You can also use XP to change Career, see the *Change Career* Endeavour, page 197.

# **Characteristic Advances**

Every Career has an Advance Scheme governing its Characteristic Advances. The Advance Scheme shows your character's 10 Characteristics, 3 marked with h, 1 marked with on a brass background, 1 marked with a on silver, and the last marked on gold.

The three marked h are Characteristics you can advance in the first level of the Career. When you enter the second level of a career, the Characteristic also becomes available, and so on.

The XP cost for Characteristic Advances is shown on the **Advancement XP Costs** table. Each Advance you purchase adds +5 to the associated Characteristic. The cost of purchasing Advances for a Characteristic increases depending on how many times you've already advanced that Characteristic.

*Example: Your Initial Agility is 27 and you purchase your first Agility Advance. Your new Agility is 32. This costs you 125 XP. To increase your Agility by a further +5 to a total of 37 costs an additional 175 XP.*

The cumulative XP cost is the cost required to increase a Characteristic if you have no prior Advances. For example, going from no Advances in Agility to +30 would cost 2,100 XP.

Note: While Weapon Skill and Ballistic Skill contain the word 'Skill', this is a historical artefact — they are Characteristics.

# **Skill Advances**

You can advance all the Skills listed for your Career level and lower. The cost in XP of a Skill Advance is shown in the **Advancement XP Costs** table, and depends on the number of Skill Advances you have already taken in that Skill.

Each Skill Advance adds +5 to your Skill level. XP Costs for Skills increase in the same way as for Characteristics.

*Example: You purchase your first Advance (+5) in Stealth and your Agility was 31, your Stealth would be 36. This costs 50 XP. Increasing this by a further +5 to 41 would cost an additional 75 XP.*

VI **SPENDING XP** The full rules for how to use your Skills can be found in **Chapter 4: Skills and Talents**. Like Characteristic Advances, there is no upper limit to the number of Skill Advances that can be purchased. The table below presents costs for even the most ambitious campaigns and improbably successful characters.

# **Purchasing Talents**

You can purchase any of the Talents listed for your current Career level as well as those from lower levels.

The rules for every Talent can be found in **Chapter 4: Skills and Talents**. Each Talent costs 100 XP, and purchasing it grants your Character access to the Talent's special rules. Some Talents can be taken multiple times, for 100 XP each time, as listed in their description.

When you purchase a Talent from your Career, remember to tick one box on the Career Advancement Tracker.

# **Advancing Careers**

The rules for increasing your Career Level, or changing to a new Career, can be found under the *Advance Career* (page 196) and *Change Career* (page 197) Endeavours. Even if you are not using the Endeavour system, these rules still apply.

# **REGIONAL EVENTS**

The GM should roll once on the Regional Event table to determine what's going on in the local area during the Characters' downtime. If there is some important event, local festival or misfortune, or a prominent piece of gossip doing the rounds that will be important to a future adventure, the GM can substitute that instead of rolling.

#### **01–02: Grand Procession**

Crowds flock to the roadside to watch as a great procession of the faithful, dedicated to a god of the Empire chosen by the GM, winds its way through the region. Warrior priests, wild figures in animal skins, chanting clergy, or solemn mourners accompany the procession, depending on the cult. Characters devoted to the deity being honoured are inspired by the spectacle, and may choose to make a single Test with Advantage during the next adventure.

#### **03–04: Unusual Mercenaries**

One or more rare mercenaries turn up in a nearby settlement looking for work: a Tilean Duellist of great repute, the so-called Birdmen of Catrazza, unemployed ogres under the command of a halfling captain, or other unlikely bands. The mercenaries will gladly train any Character in any martial Skills or Talents, at a 20% reduction in costs, should the Characters undertake a *Training* or *Unusual Learning* Endeavour.

#### **05–07: Auguries of the West Wind**

The surrounding area suffers from a severe drought. In desperation, the local lord sends for help from the Colleges of Magic in Altdorf, who dispatch a wizard with a mighty Celestial Hurricanum. As the esoteric device scours the local landscape with wind and rain, it not only terrifies the locals, but also draws the Blue Wind of *Azyr*. All Casting and Channelling Tests involving *Azyr* during the next adventure gain +1 SL.

#### **08–12: Teacher, Teacher!**

A travelling tutor sets up shop in the local square, offering to teach any and all in exchange for coin. The GM chooses an Advanced Skill. This Downtime, any Character may use the *Training* Endeavour to learn that skill, without having to use *Consult an Expert* to find a teacher.

#### **13–16: The Doktor is In**

A travelling barber surgeon arrives in the region, driving a wagon packed with medical supplies and a reclining leather chair only lightly stained with blood. Scarce and Rare Herbs and Draughts (page 314) have their Availability improved by one step during this Downtime. They will also happily perform any amputations, tooth extractions, or haircuts for free as a demonstration to draw in coin-laden crowds.

#### **17–21: Artisan Caravan**

A caravan of dwarfen artisans stops off on its journey between holds, willing to trade superior crafting materials. All Tests taken as part of a *Crafting* Endeavour during this Downtime benefit from +2 SL.

#### **22–26: Flowing Profit**

Disruption on major roads due to brigands, beastmen, or whatever threatens the locale drives trade to the waterways. All Riverfolk make an additional 50% from any *Income* Endeavour pursued during this Downtime.

#### **27–30: Opportunity Saunters Through**

Passing soldiers, well-heeled merchants, or vacationing nobles pass through the area, and those willing to curry favour reap the benefits. Burgher and Peasant Class Characters make an additional 50% from any *Income* Endeavour pursued during this Downtime.

#### **31–35: Festivities**

A celebration is called! The GM decides the nature of the event. Possibilities include a local wedding, a feast of Rhya in thanks for a bountiful harvest, or a public execution! With so many people around, any Gossip Tests made during this downtime benefit from Advantage, while Artists and Entertainers double their money from the *Income* Endeavour.

#### **36–39: Marvellous Miracle Cures**

A travelling potion salesman arrives in the region, driving a gaudy wagon bedecked with racks of mysterious liquids in vials, jars, and earthen jugs. Scarce and Rare Herbs and Draughts (page 314) have their Availability improved by one step if bought from the travelling merchant during this Downtime. For each item bought this way, the GM rolls a d10: on a 1 or 2, it is actually a Poison (page 313) of the GM's choosing.

A local lord levies warriors to form a regiment in his name. Maybe they are looking to contribute to a wider war effort, assemble a private army for their own purposes, or just keep up appearances through sponsoring a temporary military exercise. Characters from the Warrior Class gain an additional 50% from any *Income* Endeavours as they assist in training or recruiting villagers. Warriors who undertake the *Income* Endeavour count as having taken the *Combat Training* Endeavour.

#### **43–45: Travelling Players**

A colourful troupe of travelling players arrives, erecting a makeshift stage in the market square and performing tales of heroic deeds, tragic romances, and biting satires of local worthies. The local worthies swiftly have them arrested and ban all public gatherings as seditious. *Consult an Expert*, *Foment Dissent*, and *The Latest News* each require 1 additional Endeavour this Downtime as people become fearful of speaking openly.

#### **46–52: Inclement Weather**

Particularly bad weather arrives. For the next adventure, all social Skill Tests suffer a penalty of -1 SL (everyone is in a foul mood due to the rain), and food prices increase by 50% due to spoiling of stores.

#### **53–55: Religious Mania**

A brief but intense period of religious mania breaks out, perhaps due to unexplained celestial phenomena, the preaching of a radical prophet, or a beast born with the sign of one of the gods. Any Pray Tests made during this Downtime benefit from Advantage.

#### **56–59: Power Vacuum**

A prominent local leader — such as a guildmaster, village elder, or watch captain — is suddenly replaced due to illness, scandal, or looking askance at a passing witch hunter. The local political landscape is a powderkeg as factions vie for power in the wake of the change. *Foment Dissent* only requires 1 Endeavour, rather than 2, this Downtime.

#### **60–64: New Moon**

The nights are particularly dark, providing ideal cover for those who work in the shadows. Rogue Characters undertaking the *Income* Endeavour this Downtime increase the money they earn by 50%.

#### **65–68: False Silver**

To the horror of the merchants, a run of counterfeit coins plagues the locale. Characters who undertake the *Banking* Endeavour will lose 20% of money banked, and those undertaking the *Income* Endeavour similarly reduce any money earned by 20%.

#### **69–73: Tax Collector**

VI **40–42: Levy** An unexpected visit by the tax collector (with several soldiers in tow) sweeps through the settlement. Those with legal employment earn 50% less from any *Income* Endeavours due to harsh taxes, while Rogues and others who operate outside the law earn double the usual amount as opportunities for smuggling and dirty dealing abound.

#### **74–81: Crime Crackdown**

The Watch is cracking down hard. Rogues can get no funds from the *Income* Endeavour, and if Rogues undertake a *Banking*  Endeavour, they may only select a high-risk deposit, as legitimate banking houses won't handle dirty money right now.

#### **82–88: Glorious Weather**

Particularly sunny weather arrives. For the next adventure, all social Skill Tests suffer a penalty of -1 SL (everyone is in a foul mood due to the heat). Dry roads make trade easier, however, increasing the availability of all non-Exotic goods by one step.

#### **89–91: Local Crop Failure**

Food becomes exceedingly scarce and many folk suffer terribly. Characters from the Peasant Class cannot undertake the *Income*  Endeavour, and food prices are doubled in the area for the duration of the next adventure.

#### **92–94: Malicious Malady**

The Washers Guild are hard at work as the Bloody Flux sweeps through town. Each Character must pass an **Easy (+4 SL) Endurance** Test or contract the Bloody Flux (page 180) on a failure.

#### **95–97: Riots**

Due to a sudden tax increase, public scandal, or the ravings of a deranged prophet, the common folk are furious at the high and mighty! Characters of the Courtier Class cannot take the *Income* Endeavour as, no matter how many bodyguards they hire, it isn't even vaguely safe for them to be abroad on business. Players attempting a *Foment Dissent* Endeavour benefit from Advantage on any related Tests.

#### **98–00: Under Suspicion**

The party's irregular movements or sudden wealth has drawn suspicion. The Characters become acquainted with the local magistrate, and must forfeit one Endeavour either laying low or proving their innocence. Characters in the Rogue class cannot use the *Income* Endeavour until after their next adventure.

# **CHARACTER EVENTS**

Each player rolls d100 on the Character Events table to determine what's been happening to their Character during the Downtime. Some Events may impact Endeavours and other Downtime activities, while others have effects that linger for your next adventure.

#### **01–04: Inspiration Strikes**

Through diligent study or a flash of sudden inspiration, you make a breakthrough at improving your skills. Gain 50 XP.

#### **05–09: Unexpected Esteem**

Someone you helped in the past has a chance to repay your efforts. The precise nature of the reward should be appropriate to your past deeds, and the NPCs you have assisted during play or in your back story. The tokens of gratitude may vary from a single high-quality item, to a purse of silver (which will be available at the start of the next adventure). Of course, not all gifts are what they appear and the GM may attach an awkward complication to this sudden windfall.

#### **10–14: Somebody Else's Problem**

Whether through luck, good timing, or a fortunate misunderstanding, somebody else ends up dealing with a problem that would normally have landed on your doorstep. You find yourself with some unexpectedly free time. Gain 1 additional Endeavour to use this Downtime.

#### **15–19: A Quiet Week**

Nothing catches fire, the tax collector passes you over, and nobody accuses you of witchcraft. By the standards of the Empire, it has been an extraordinarily peaceful week. During the next adventure, you may ignore the first Fumble or Minor Miscast you would suffer.

#### **20–24: A Good Day's Work**

Fortune smiles upon your labours. Perhaps a satisfied customer recommends your services, your master is unusually generous with your wages, or your latest mark was particularly gullible. Should you take the *Income* Endeavour this Downtime, increase your Standing by 3 for the purposes of determining how much you earn.

#### **25–26: Is that an Original von Klapper?**

A bespectacled noble passing you on the street suddenly stops and exclaims with excitement. They are convinced that one of your Trappings (determined by the GM) is in fact a rare and important example of a particular artisan's work. You may immediately sell the Trapping to the hapless collector for twice its listed value.

#### **27–29: Morr's Embrace**

A relative, old friend, or mysterious benefactor dies and leaves you some money in their will. Gain some coin appropriate to your Status — Brass: 20 brass pennies; Silver: 10 silver shillings; Gold: 2 gold crowns. This windfall is received before completing Endeavours, so must be spent or banked before jealous family members, opportunistic cutpurses, or hidden burial fees whisk it away; see **Money to Burn** below.

#### **30–34: Forewarned is Forearmed**

You are given a cryptic omen by a dream, two-shilling mystic, or Celestial wizard you encounter on the street. During your next adventure, your maximum Fortune points are increased by 1.

#### **35–41: What a Bargain!**

While browsing the local markets, you come across an item tucked away in a stall that has clearly been overlooked by the merchant, who doesn't know the value of what they have. You may purchase a single Trapping for half its usual price.

#### **42–47: Pedlar**

A wizened, well-travelled pedlar who likes to gossip passes through. For the price of a flagon of ale (6 d) you may attempt the *Latest News* Endeavours without using one of your Endeavours.

#### **48–51: A Useful Connection**

Through your labours or a social encounter, you find yourself in the home of an accomplished artisan or scholar. Without requiring one of your Endeavours, you may choose one of the following options: undertake the Commission Endeavour, or undertake the Research Lore Endeavour, with Advantage on any associated Tests.

#### **52–56: Soothsayer**

You discover a small tent in a corner of the local market square, from which wafts the scent of incense and bitter herbs. A wizened crone beckons you inside and offers to read your fortune. For the price of 1 shilling, you may acquire the *Doomed* Talent, if you do not already have it, or otherwise roll on the table of *Doomings* (page 118) replacing your previous result with the new result. Should you try to locate the tent again so your companions can get a reading, you are mysteriously unable to find it.

The belongings of a recently deceased neighbour are auctioned, including a dusty, old book of unknown origin. If you are able to pay the 2 GC asking price, the book is yours! Beyond any other secrets the book may reveal, as determined by the GM, you (or any other Character of your choice who can read) may undertake the *Research Lore* Endeavour without using one of your Endeavours, making any required Test with Advantage.

#### **60–67: Oi! You spilled my pint!**

While carousing in a tavern, browsing the markets, attending a cult rite, or any appropriate activity, you run afoul of a hotheaded member of a local guild, criminal enterprise, or other organisation. The GM determines the faction in question and, during the next adventure, you suffer Disadvantage on social Tests with members of that group.

#### **68–74: Onerous Obligations**

A family wedding, guild meeting, temple festival, or some other unavoidable obligation demands your attention. By the time every duty has been discharged and every long-winded acquaintance has finished bending your ear, little time remains. You have 1 fewer Endeavour to use this Downtime.

#### **75–79: Impounded**

A zealous official decides one of your possessions requires inspection, a new licence, or proof of ownership. Recovering it means navigating the Empire's labyrinthine bureaucracy. Spend 1 Endeavour reclaiming one of your Trappings (chosen by the GM), or begin the next adventure without it.

#### **80–84: Ranald's Wrath**

Whether you cheated the wrong gambler or laughed at the wrong joke, the trickster god seems to have taken an interest in you… for the worse. Your clothes catch on each nail you pass, every ale you order is sour, and a pigeon relieves itself on your hat three times in one day. During the next adventure, your maximum Fortune points are decreased by 1.

#### **85–90: Scandalous Rumours**

VI **57–59: Arcane Auction** Through no fault of your own — or perhaps entirely through your own doing — you become embroiled in a local scandal. Awkward conversations and sidelong glances follow wherever you go. During the next adventure, you cannot spend Fortune on social Tests.

#### **91–92: Betrayed!**

A friend, family member, or ally turns on you. Decide with the GM who betrayed you and why. During the next adventure, the betrayer or their agents may complicate your plans at least once. Whether or not you can make amends in some way is up to the GM.

#### **93–97: Accused Ally**

One of your allies (GM's choice) is implicated in a crime. A Character with a Law Guild Licence can use up one of their Endeavours to defend the ally on a successful **Average (+2 SL) Lore (Law)** Test. Otherwise, 3 Endeavours spent by the party will exonerate their ally (or, if guilty, concoct a convincing alibi). If the ally is freed, gain a *Major Favour* (see **Do Me a Favour** on page 200) for future use. If not, the ally faces punishment — fines, imprisonment, or even execution, as the GM sees fit.

#### **98–00: They're a Witch!**

A fearful local or an enemy with ulterior motives accuses you of witchcraft. You may spend an Endeavour and make a **Difficult (-1 SL) Charm** Test to attempt to clear your name. If you fail the Test or do not attempt to clear your name, a witch hunter takes interest in you. The GM may have them show up at inopportune moments during your adventures until you successfully clear your name. Killing the witch hunter will only confirm people's suspicions and set their allies upon you.

# **MONEY TO BURN**

After you've rolled on the **Character Events Table**, you can spend the money earned during your last adventures using the normal rules. After completing your Endeavours, all money held by your Character is considered spent. All of it. The silver lifted from a Burgher's purse? Gone. The booty liberated from pirates? Also gone. That Troll hoard you raided? Gone, too. All of it. What happened to it? It was spent, stolen, drunk, gambled, used for repairs, to pay off debts or taxes, given as a charitable donations or votive offerings, spent on bribes, or used in whatever other way you prefer. You should concoct the best story for what happens to those funds, as it explains a lot about your Character. If you want to keep some of your hard-won coin to use in the future, you should undertake the *Banking* Endeavour on page 197. If you want money at the beginning of the next adventure, honestly, or otherwise, depending on your Career, you should undertake the Income Endeavour on page 199.

# **ENDEAVOURS**

Activities Characters undertake between adventures are known as Endeavours. You may choose one Endeavour per week if you are not adventuring, up to a maximum of three Endeavours in each period of downtime.

# **Duties & Responsibilities**

You may lose one or more of your Endeavours due to your Career or Species.

# **Burdens of Power**

*'The common folk will never understand my burdens. The sheer weight of the duties I bear. The guilty will not judge themselves, and there are always so many villages to cleanse, for our enemies are legion.'*

– Hulbart Voggentraas, Witch Hunter

As you climb the ranks of your career, you accrue expectations and responsibilities that cannot be avoided. Noble Lords must see to the affairs of their estates, Guildmasters must oversee their guild's work, and Watch Captains must watch their watchmen. If you neglect your responsibilities, you will soon lose status amongst your peers.

If you have reached the highest Level of your Career and do not take the *Income* Endeavour — representing the time spent attending to the demands of your position — you must still make the Earning Test (see *Income*, page 199). On a success, you do not lose any status, but also do not earn any coin. On a failure, your Standing permanently decreases by 1 within your current Status Tier. Each time you later take the *Income*  Endeavour, your Standing increases by 1, up to the maximum for your current Career Level.

If losing Standing this way would reduce your Standing to 0, you drop a level within your Career; reverting from the fourth level to the third. This costs no Experience Points, acting as a free Career change, albeit downwards.

With this step backwards in your Career Path, you will now have a lower Status, and will earn less money should you perform future *Income* Endeavours. See **Chapter 3: Class and Careers** for more about Status. The loss in status does not remove any Advances acquired while you were at the higher Career level.

If you wish to reclaim your lost Career level, you must once again pay the XP cost to re-enter the higher level of your Career and retake your 'proper' place in society.

### **Elves and Yenlui**

*'There are times when I can barely stand Altdorf. It is not the stench — though that is abhorrent — it is not the noise, either — it is just that you are all so very ugly to look upon, I feel that I must gaze at something beautiful for a time or I know that I shall start screaming, and never stop.'*

– Irlianmaris Ellarel, Asur Envoy

The spiritual philosophy known as *yenlui* is common to elven cultures. Mastering this discipline is essential for an elf to maintain their soul's balance between darkness and light. Excess darkness can lead an elf into acts of depravity and cruelty, whereas too much light leads to world-weariness and distraction.

Because of this need to maintain a sense of spiritual and psychological balance, an elf Character must spend one of their Endeavours ensuring that they keep control of their psyches, using at least some of their time away from adventuring to meditate, pray, and attain a state of harmonious *yenlui*.

The *High Elf Players Guide* gives more detailed information on maintaining *yenlui*, and what the consequences are of failing to keep it in balance. If using those rules, the elf Character does not need to spend one of their Endeavours on monitoring *yenlui*  — either they will be keeping it under control as a result of their behaviour, or suffering as a result of being imbalanced.

# **Common Endeavours**

Below you will find details of the Endeavours any Character may undertake between adventures. Note that the GM can decide whether a particular Endeavour is available, given the location and circumstances of the Characters during their period of Downtime.

#### **Advance Career**

To advance in your Career, you need to spend some time rubbing shoulders with the right people, impressing your superiors, or removing those that block your rise with a discreet knife to the back. Once you have filled enough boxes on the Career Advancement Tracker on your Character Sheet by aquiring Trappings, Talents, and Advances, choose this Endeavour and spend 100 XP to move up a level in your Career. You must tick 10 boxes before moving up to level two, 12 more for level three, and 14 more for level four, as shown on your Character Sheet.

# **Animal Training**

You spend time training one of your animals. Make an **Average (+2 SL) Animal Training** Test. If successful, add one skill to your animal from the *Trained* Creature Trait (see page 363).

All the money remaining at the end of your 'Between Adventures' phase is lost before the next adventure begins, unless it is saved with this Endeavour. You have two options when taking the Banking Endeavour:

#### **Invest with Banking House**

- 0 Gold and Silver Status Characters only.
- 0 Determine risk and reward choose a number between 1 and 10 (or roll 1d10). This is the rate of interest you will accrue, and also how risky the investment is.
- 0 Withdrawing funds requires another *Banking* Endeavour, either immediately after the initial Endeavour, or after subsequent adventures. Roll 1d100: if you roll equal to or less than the risk and reward number, the venture has failed and you have lost all of your money. If you roll over the interest rate, you receive your initial funds, plus the interest accrued.

#### **Stash the Loot**

- 0 Hide/bury/give to someone for safekeeping a high risk option available to all Characters
- 0 Withdraw money from a stash before the start of an adventure without undertaking an Endeavour. Roll 1d100: if you roll 10 or lower, your stash has been found and you have lost all your money, otherwise you receive your initial funds. If you successfully withdraw your funds they are available for you when you start your next adventure, in addition to any funds secured via an *Income* Endeavour.

**Example:** *Gerhard and Ulli's courage in the face of a horde of goblins (not to mention some judicious looting) has earned them a haul worth 10 gold crowns each. The ever-prudent Gerhard decides to place his money with the esteemed Fooger's Counting House in Marienburg. The GM rolls 1d10 with a result of 6. Gerhard will earn 6% interest on his deposit (12 silver shillings, see page 296), and the investment will fail if he rolls 6 or less on d100 when he tries to make a withdrawal.*

*Ulli — a gambler at heart — buries his money near a milemarker on the road to Wrecker's Point. He doesn't need to make an Endeavour to get access to his loot, but tragically he rolls 07 when he tries to recover it, and finds nothing but freshly disturbed earth, an empty chest, and a whole heap of regret.*

### **Change Career**

VI **Banking** Time spent on this Endeavour represents making the appropriate introductions, doling out bribes, acquiring permits and anything else needed to start a new Career. Work out the particulars with your GM — switching from Soldier to Sailor might be as simple as being press-ganged into the navy, while switching from a Rat Catcher to become a Noble is all but impossible. Additionally, not every Career is available to every Species. Assuming your GM agrees, you may pay 100 XP to change to a Career within your current Class, or 200 XP to change to a Career in another Class.

# **Combat Training**

You spend time training with the weapons you know, honing your skills for the day when it matters most. After undertaking this Endeavour, you may reroll one Melee or Ranged Skill Test in your next adventure. You can take this Endeavour multiple times if you wish.

# **Commission**

So, you wish to own one of von Meinkopt's legendary repeating pistols, or commission a custom shield with a personalised crest representing your greatest victory? The *Commission* Endeavour allows you to acquire Exotic Trappings and other bespoke items unavailable in the Empire's shops. Such commissions take time.

If you wish to commission an Exotic item, you must first find someone capable of making or acquiring it. If you do not already have such a contact, undertake *Consult an Expert* to locate one. Once you have found a suitable craftsperson or supplier, place your order and pay the item's cost. It will be ready for collection after your next adventure.

Each *Commission* Endeavour allows you to order one Exotic item. Additional items require additional *Commission* Endeavours.

# **Consult an Expert**

You may undertake the *Consult an Expert* Endeavour either to facilitate another Endeavour—such as *Commission*, *Training*, or *Unusual Learning* — or to obtain specific knowledge.

First, you must locate an expert, unless you already know one. Make a **Challenging (+0 SL) Gossip** Test. On a success, you find a genuine expert. On a failure, you instead find the loudest self-proclaimed authority in the area, and the quality of their advice is up to the GM.

Experts rarely give their time freely. They may require payment, or ask you to perform one or more Favours (see **Do Me a Favour!**, page 200), which can be called upon during a future adventure or 'bought off ' by spending Endeavours between future adventures. The GM determines what, if anything, the expert demands.

The information you receive depends on both the expert consulted and the questions you ask. Academics know the history of their subjects, wizards of the Colleges of Magic understand magic and mystical artefacts — especially those of their own Order — Apothecary Generals can identify even the rarest poisons, and Verenan scholars recall countless historical events. Every expert presents their knowledge through the lens of their own experience and biases.

If you use this Endeavour to gain knowledge, rather than support another Endeavour, you also gain an Expert Reroll (record this on your Character Sheet). You may use this reroll once on a Test directly related to the knowledge you gained before the end of your next adventure.

# **Crafting**

Crafting allows you to make any trapping from **Chapter 11: Consumer Guide** (or anything else the GM allows) if you have the correct Trade Skills. To do this, you require appropriate Trade Tools, raw materials, and access to an appropriate workshop. In general, the raw materials to create the trapping of your choice will cost a quarter of the Trapping's list price.

To craft the Trapping, attempt an Extended Trade Test, with a Difficulty set by the GM, typically using the Trapping's Availability as a loose guide.

# **CUSTOM GEAR**

With a GM's permission, a Character may undertake two (or more) consecutive Commission Endeavours to acquire even more unusual items: personal commissions from the Engineering Guild, a rare and virulent poison, or a magically ensorcelled item. The prices for such things generally range from 'very high' to 'staggering' and may well involve a Favour or two (see **Do Me a Favour!** on page 200) or even an entire adventure to acquire raw materials.

The number of SL required to succeed at the Test is also set by the GM, using the listed price for the trapping in the Consumer Guide as a reference.

The SL required is further modified by the Qualities or Flaws you work into the Trapping. Each Flaw halves the SL required, and each Quality adds +5 (worked out after halving for Flaws). Each Endeavour you spend Crafting allows you to make one roll towards your Extended Test. Unfinished work can be kept in whatever you have that amounts to lodgings, or carried around with you as you adventure, should that be possible.

#### **Foment Dissent**

If you wish to stir civil unrest against a specific individual, group, or institution, undertake the *Foment Dissent* Endeavour. By spreading rumours, leading meetings, and exploiting local grievances, you gradually turn public opinion against your chosen target. This takes time, requiring two Endeavours (and also counts as an *Income* Endeavour if you are an Agitator).

First, make an **Average (+2 SL) Gossip** Test to identify the local power players. If successful, make a Charm Test, with the Difficulty determined by how unpopular the target is. Turning a mob against a hated noble may be Easy (+4 SL), while stirring outrage against a respected Shallyan hospice could be Hard (-2 SL). If either Test fails, the Endeavour fails.

If successful, you can attempt a Charm Test during your next adventure to rally a mob against your chosen target. The GM sets the Difficulty according to the circumstances. On a success, an angry crowd gathers to heckle, threaten, and harass the target. An Impressive or Astounding Success may spark a riot or even an attempted burning. Failure means the crowd refuses to act, while a particularly bad failure may alert the target or their supporters.

Once you have successfully fomented dissent, you may try to turn the mob against a different target during the same adventure, though the Charm Test is two Difficulty levels harder.

Most people resume their usual employment between bouts of adventuring. Bounty Hunters hunt bounties, Riverwardens ward rivers, Troll Slayers slay trolls — in the modern Empire, some even rely on a lucrative skill not featured in their job title. Wealthier Characters, such as Nobles and Merchants, might collect a stipend by (briefly) indulging their family, or collecting interest on their investments and business ventures. Roguish types have any number of means to acquire funds, though it's best not to ask for details.

Take the *Income* Endeavour to represent your Character working in their chosen Career. Make an Average (+2 SL) Test using the *Earning Skill* listed in bold italics in the first Level of your Career. On a success, you earn income based on the **Status**  of your current Career Level, as shown below.

# **EARNED INCOME**

| Status Tier | Income                                   |
|-------------|------------------------------------------|
| Brass       | 2d10 brass pennies per Standing Level    |
| Silver      | 1d10 silver shillings per Standing Level |
| Gold        | 1 gold crown per Standing Level          |

On a failure, work is scarce, expenses mount, or some new tax eats into your wages. Roll your earnings as normal, but halve the amount received, rounding down.

Certain results on the **Events Tables** may directly affect your income. The money you get from an *Income* Endeavour is given to you after your money from the last adventure is spent (see **Money to Burn**).

*Example: Molli needs to earn some coin, so she takes to the streets to do some good, honest thieving. As a Thief, her Earning Skill is Stealth (Urban), so she makes an Average (+2 SL) Stealth (Urban) Test. Her Stealth (Urban) is 46, but she rolls 83 — a failure even with the +2 SL bonus. Ranald has turned his back on her, it seems. Molli is a Level 1 Prowler with Brass 3 Status, so she rolls 6d10 for her income. The result is 34, halved to 17 because of the failed Test. She earns just 17 brass pennies this Endeavour, and makes a mental note to leave an offering at the next shrine to Ranald in the hope of regaining the Trickster God's favour.*

#### **Invent!**

VI **Income** The *Invent* Endeavour allows you to create entirely new Trappings by combining the properties of two or more existing ones. A pair of Pistols might become a Repeating Pistol, or Pigeons combined with Bombs might produce Pigeon Bombs. The exact effects of any invention are ultimately determined by the GM.

Inventing is a two-stage process: Plan and Build.

**Plan**: Decide which Trappings you wish to combine, then spend 1 Endeavour creating your blueprints. Make a Trade (Engineer) Test, with the Difficulty determined by the GM according to how ambitious or outlandish the invention is. On a success, the plans are complete. Each SL scored grants +1 SL when you later attempt to Build the invention.

**Build**: Complete the invention by undertaking either a *Crafting*  or *Commission* Endeavour.

- 0 Crafting: You build the invention yourself. You must possess the Skills required to create each component for example, combining Pigeons and Bombs requires Animal Training and Trade (Explosives). The Crafting Test is always Very Hard (–3 SL), modified by any bonus gained from the Plan stage. The raw materials cost twice the combined listed price of the Trappings and have an Availability equal to the rarest component.
- 0 Commission: You hire someone else to build the invention. You may first need to undertake *Consult an Expert* to locate a suitable engineer. Resolve the *Commission* Endeavour as normal, except the work costs six times the combined listed price of the Trappings.

The GM has the final say on the rules and capabilities of every invention.

# **HARDLY WORKING**

If you do not take the *Income* Endeavour during Downtime, you neglect your Career and earn no income. You begin the next adventure with just a single coin appropriate to your Status Tier: 1 brass penny, 1 silver shilling, or 1 gold crown.

For Characters at the highest Level of their Career, neglecting the demands of their position has more lasting consequences. See **Burdens of Power**, page 196.

#### **The Latest News**

This Endeavour allows you to learn the latest news from afar. Attempt a **Challenging (+0 SL) Gossip** Test. On a success, you hear an interesting rumour, with your SL determining how accurate and detailed it is. The news may relate to an upcoming adventure, events elsewhere in the region, or anything else the GM considers appropriate. If you fail particularly badly (an Impressive Failure) you learn something false you are convinced is true. To facilitate this, the GM may want to make the Gossip Test in secret on your behalf.

# **Raise Reputation**

The most reliable way to increase your social standing and exert influence in the Empire is through a gratuitous and conspicuous demonstration of wealth. From funding lavish feasts to buying the entire tavern a round, nothing impresses quite like spending money where everyone can see it.

Spend coin equal to your maximum Earnings (see page 199). For example, if your maximum Earnings are 4d10 brass pennies, you must spend 40d. Your Standing increases by +1 until your next Downtime, unless your reputation is somehow damaged during an adventure.

# **Research Lore**

This Endeavour allows you to seek greater knowledge concerning a specific subject, such as the site of a battle, a famed historical event, or an individual. Your Character must have access to an appropriate storehouse of lore, such as a library, the annals of a dwarf hold, the records of a guild, or a temple of Verena.

Attempt an **Average (+2 SL) Research** Test. If you succeed, you learn one piece of interesting, useful, or hidden knowledge about the subject researched, and each SL adds another piece of relevant information. If you fail particularly badly (an Impressive or Astounding Failure), you learn something false that you are convinced is true. To facilitate this, the GM may want to make the Research Test in secret on your behalf.

# **DO ME A FAVOUR!**

The powerful often find it preferable to be owed favours rather than money. They are already wealthy, and a favour can be a superior investment: help a student today, call in a favour from a successful Lawyer years later. Favours may be referred to as 'boons', 'services', or 'obligations', but they all amount to the same.

Favours come in three levels: Minor, Major, and Significant. Regardless of a Favour's level, in almost all cases, the person owed the Favour will take into account the capabilities and sensibilities of the person they're asking. There's no point in demanding that a Priestess of Shallya assassinate a merchant, for example, even if she does owe you a Significant Favour. That being said, Favours are ripe for NPCs to exploit in order to put pressure on your moral compass.

- 0 **A Minor Favour** represents a simple task that can be completed in no more than a few hours. Perhaps you need to volunteer at a Shallyan mercy-house or put in a good word for someone. With the GM's permission, you may undertake an Endeavour to pay off this favour.
- 0 **A Major Favour** is a more time-consuming or risky undertaking. It could take up to several weeks to achieve and may involve travel. Perhaps you are needed to act as lookout on a string of burglaries, convince the Stevedores' and Teamsters' Guild to back down from a guild war, or transport a package to a different city. With the GM's permission, you may undertake two or more consecutive Endeavours to pay off this favour.
- 0 **A Significant Favour** is relatively rare and will almost certainly involve risking life and limb. Months of travel may be involved, and major violence is likely. Perhaps you will be asked to eliminate a rival gang boss, wipe out a goblin encampment, or instigate a riot. Significant Favours cannot be paid off via Endeavours; they are roleplayed out as full adventures.

In the Old World, breaking one's sworn word carries significant social ramifications. No-one trusts oath breakers, meaning your Status is reduced by 1 Tier (see page @@). Powerful and dangerous folks tend to take it very, very poorly if someone refuses a Favour owed them, so bloody consequences may follow.

Use this Endeavour to observe a potential target and gain advantages during future criminal enterprises. A Charlatan may study a merchant in order to better impersonate them during a future scam, or a Thief may spend time watching the comings and goings of a warehouse's guards to get an idea of their patrol routes.

Attempt a **Challenging (+0 SL) Perception** Test, though the difficulty may be modified for especially easy or difficult marks. If you succeed, you may gain Advantage on a Test concerning your mark once during your next adventure. Note this on your Character Sheet. You can take this Endeavour multiple times if you wish. Further, the GM should provide information (or disinformation!) concerning your mark according to the SL you scored.

### **Training**

This Endeavour allows you to train a Skill or Characteristic outside your Career. In addition to the normal XP cost, you must also pay a tutor. Training in Basic Skills and Characteristics costs a number of pennies equal to the XP cost of the Advance. Advanced Skills cost twice that amount. For more on Advances, see page 191.

VI **Study a Mark** You should be able to find a tutor for most skills, especially in a large city such as Altdorf. For some specialist Skills, however, you may first need to undertake *Consult an Expert* to locate a suitable teacher — those willing to teach Pick Lock, for example, rarely advertise their services.

> Some Skills, particularly certain Lore Skills, can only be learned at appropriate institutions, while others are outright forbidden in the Empire. Finding a tutor able (and willing) to teach proscribed subjects, such as Necromancy or those related to the Ruinous Powers, may itself become an adventure. Such tutors usually demand far more than the usual fee, often requiring a Significant Favour (see **Do Me a Favour!**, page 200).

#### **Unusual Learning**

It is one thing to pick up a Skill that can be readily practised, quite another to awaken a Talent. This Endeavour allows you to learn the first level of a Talent outside your Career. It cannot be used to gain additional levels in a Talent you already possess.

You must first find a suitable tutor. In most large cities this is straightforward, but particularly obscure Talents, or those sought far from civilisation, may require you to undertake *Consult an Expert* first. Once you have found a tutor, training costs 4d10 silver shillings.
